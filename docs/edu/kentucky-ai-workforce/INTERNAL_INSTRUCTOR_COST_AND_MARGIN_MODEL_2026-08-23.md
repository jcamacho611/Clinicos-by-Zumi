# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness — Internal Instructor Cost and Margin Stress Model

Status: **internal planning model only**. This is not a submitted quote, compensation agreement, employment agreement, subcontract, prevailing-wage determination, or representation of SCWDB-approved pricing.

## Purpose

Klinikos should not price senior professional instructional time as if the program were a low-cost prerecorded course. The customer-facing Appendix B rates must recover the real cost of live delivery, preparation, review, reporting, platform support, travel exposure, and senior occupational expertise while remaining commercially attractive.

This model is deliberately assumption-driven. Replace planning assumptions with documented compensation arrangements before final pricing approval.

## Delivery roles

### Applied AI / platform lead

Core responsibilities include:
- applied AI instruction;
- prompting and verification;
- responsible-AI boundaries;
- Zumi / Klinikos EDU demonstrations;
- workflow-automation exercises;
- learner support;
- instructor calibration;
- product/program coordination;
- selected assessment and evidence review.

### Senior healthcare occupational instructor / SME

Core responsibilities may include:
- healthcare workflow context;
- professional boundaries;
- patient-communication scenarios;
- privacy/confidentiality context;
- administrative and operational healthcare use cases;
- instructor review of occupational exercises;
- scenario calibration;
- escalation/human-authority interpretation.

The staffing model should allow one senior healthcare professional to lead or co-teach a Healthcare cohort while a second healthcare professional is available for curriculum review, backup coverage, or selected sessions. Do not assume two senior healthcare professionals must be present for every delivery hour unless the final curriculum requires it.

## Operational implication

The premium healthcare instructional advantage supports the current higher-value pricing posture, but the program should not accidentally require two senior professionals for every small cohort. The strongest commercial/delivery configuration is generally:

1. one primary live instructor owns the session;
2. the Applied AI lead owns the AI/platform portions;
3. the occupational professional owns the pathway-specific professional context;
4. co-teaching is used where it materially improves learner value;
5. a second professional provides backup, selected modules, review, or calibration rather than automatic duplicate staffing;
6. cohort size is managed so live quality and economics both remain defensible.

## Recommended internal cohort assumptions

Until SCWDB publishes or confirms a required class-size rule, use these planning assumptions only:

- **Preferred live-remote Accelerator:** 20–30 participants.
- **Preferred in-person Accelerator:** 18–25 participants.
- **Small-cohort caution zone:** fewer than 15 completions where senior co-teaching is required.
- **Large-cohort review zone:** above 30 participants; add facilitation/support capacity or demonstrate why instructional quality remains adequate.

These are internal planning assumptions, not SCWDB requirements.

## In-person day-rate check

Current working customer-facing in-person line: **$3,500 instructor delivery day plus pre-approved travel at actual reasonable cost**.

This line should be treated as incremental when Appendix B permits it and when the per-completion rate alone does not recover the additional burden of onsite deployment.

For premium healthcare co-teaching, $3,500 should not automatically be interpreted as covering two senior professionals plus preparation. If the RFP requires one day-rate line only, the final commercial narrative must clearly define what the line represents and ensure participant rates recover the rest of the staffing cost.

## Implementation fee stress check

Current working one-time implementation/customization figure: **$48,000**.

Internally allocate this fee to work that is genuinely fixed or front-loaded:
- program governance and kickoff;
- five-pathway finalization;
- Career Readiness finalization;
- institutional configuration;
- reporting/data dictionary;
- instructor guides;
- assessment/rubric calibration;
- accessibility remediation;
- instructor calibration;
- demo/representative materials;
- launch QA;
- project management;
- 30-day implementation readiness.

Do not use the implementation fee as a hidden subsidy for structurally unprofitable cohort economics. Variable delivery must remain viable on its own under realistic class sizes.

## Margin targets

These are internal management targets, not customer-facing claims.

For live-delivery services, evaluate:
- **below 30% contribution margin:** generally requires restructuring or a documented strategic reason;
- **30–45%:** viable but sensitive to travel, no-shows, delayed payment, and extra review labor;
- **45–60%:** preferred range for a differentiated program with senior professional instruction and software support;
- **above 60%:** attractive, but verify that instructor quality, support, and participant outcomes are not being underfunded.

Contribution margin is not corporate profit. Corporate overhead, insurance, legal/compliance, product development, sales, taxes, and working-capital cost remain outside this simplified view unless expressly allocated.

## Pricing posture

The proposal should communicate best value rather than bargain-basement pricing.

SCWDB would be purchasing a bundled capability that can include:
- live applied-AI instruction;
- occupational professional instruction;
- Klinikos EDU;
- Zumi exercises;
- applied scenarios;
- human verification;
- assessment and rubric review;
- attendance/completion evidence;
- reporting;
- curriculum maintenance;
- accessibility support;
- implementation/configuration;
- in-person and live-remote delivery.

The strongest value argument is that SCWDB receives these capabilities through one accountable prime instead of separately procuring trainers, occupational SMEs, an LMS/training platform, assessment infrastructure, reporting infrastructure, and implementation support.

No final price should depend on treating senior professional labor as free.
