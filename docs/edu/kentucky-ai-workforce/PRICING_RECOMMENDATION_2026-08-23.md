# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness — Internal Pricing Recommendation

Status: **internal working recommendation only.** Do not submit until reconciled against the latest RFP, September 4 addenda/Q&A, staffing costs, travel assumptions, qualification/teaming costs, and verified working-capital capacity.

## Market context checked August 23, 2026

Published live AI-training benchmarks reviewed for price sanity include:

- Carter Simmons: $3,500 remote / $4,500 on-site for up to 20 participants, approximately $175/$225 per person for a short 60–90 minute workshop.
- Certstaffix: published live instructor-led AI courses around $365 for half/full-day business AI offerings and higher for multi-day technical programs.
- Data Trainers: $2,500/day for up to 10 learners plus per-student charges above 10; advanced AI carries a premium and volume discounts are offered.
- Digital Workshop Center: $395 per participant for AI Fundamentals for the Workplace.
- Next Gear Labs: $297 per participant for a 2–3 hour hands-on workshop.
- Secusysts: $650 per person/day for live online AI/automation training.
- Standin: $5,000 for a four-hour virtual workshop and $10,000 for a six-hour in-person workshop, travel separate.
- Cleveland State University continuing education: $399 per participant for a substantially longer instructor-facilitated AI workforce course.

These benchmarks are not direct substitutes for the SCWDB scope, but they show that $200 per participant is not an appropriate ceiling for a 6–8 hour industry accelerator that also includes platform access, applied practice, assessment, attendance/completion evidence, reporting, curriculum maintenance, accessibility, and governance.

## Recommended proposal posture

Use a **fixed implementation/customization amount** plus **per-completed-participant rates** responsive to Appendix B. Include ordinary Klinikos EDU platform access and completion evidence in participant pricing so the proposal remains predictable.

Do not charge separately for a basic certificate of completion. A separate credential fee creates friction and can make the proposal look transactional when completion evidence is part of the required service.

## Commercial guardrails

Do not reduce rates merely to make the headline number smaller. Before any concession, decide what Klinikos receives in exchange, such as:

- higher confirmed volume;
- larger cohort minimums;
- remote-first delivery;
- reduced customization burden;
- predictable scheduling windows;
- prompt invoice acceptance/payment terms;
- fewer on-site deployments;
- SCWDB-provided facilities/technology support;
- multi-year committed ordering structure where legally permitted.

Never trade margin for an unsupported promise of future volume.

## Negotiation strategy

1. Submit a clear responsive Appendix B schedule, not a range.
2. Keep implementation visibly separate from participant delivery.
3. Make volume discounts meaningful but not destructive.
4. Include ordinary platform/completion evidence to reduce procurement friction.
5. Keep travel and unusual on-site requirements explicit.
6. If SCWDB signals a lower budget, restructure scope/economics rather than reflexively cutting every rate.
7. Protect Klinikos pre-existing IP and do not price a transfer of platform ownership into the training fee.
8. Cost any qualification/subcontracting structure before final rates are approved.
