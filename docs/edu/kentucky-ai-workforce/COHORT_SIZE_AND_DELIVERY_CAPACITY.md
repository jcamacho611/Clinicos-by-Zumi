# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness — Cohort Size & Delivery Capacity

Status: **proposal-development operating assumption.** These values are proposed for bid planning and must be reconciled against the September 4 addenda, final instructor bench, approved accessibility/accommodation needs, and negotiated contract terms. They are not SCWDB-established class-size requirements.

## Why cohort size matters

SCWDB requires the respondent to identify recommended, minimum, and maximum class sizes for live-remote and in-person delivery. The training is intentionally hands-on: participants must practice, verify outputs, protect privacy, complete required activities, and produce evidence that an instructor can review. A webinar-sized class would weaken the exact interaction and evidence model the proposal is selling.

External benchmarks support keeping highly interactive synchronous training relatively small:

- AHRQ's published Practice Facilitator training case study reports a maximum of 20 learners, an ideal operating range of 12–15, and an instructor view that roughly 7–8 was optimal for the highest interaction in an online setting.
- University of the Highlands and Islands guidance recommends roughly 12–15 learners for highly interactive synchronous tutor-led sessions and up to 20 for seminar-style synchronous sessions.
- ATD's 2025 State of the Industry reports a median of 20 and a mode of 15 learners for live instructor-led classroom training.
- Research summarized by ERIC on online class size found instructors perceived about 15.9 learners as optimal for the highest level of interaction and about 18.9 for the course's observed interaction level.

These are directional benchmarks, not SCWDB rules.

## Proposed class-size schedule

### Service A — AI Industry Accelerator (6–8 hours, applied occupational practice)

| Modality | Minimum planned cohort | Recommended cohort | Maximum with one lead instructor | Scaling rule |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Live remote | 8 | 12–15 | 18 | Above 18 requires an approved second facilitator/producer or a second cohort so practice and instructor evidence review remain credible. |
| In person | 8 | 12–16 | 20 | Above 20 requires an approved second facilitator or cohort split, subject to room/device/accessibility conditions. |

**Why tighter than a lecture:** every participant must complete applied tasks, receive interaction opportunities, and generate completion evidence. Healthcare and other pathway exercises also require instructor judgment around verification, privacy, safety/employer boundaries, and escalation.

### Service B — AI-Powered Career Readiness (2–3 hours)

| Modality | Minimum planned cohort | Recommended cohort | Maximum with one lead instructor | Scaling rule |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Live remote | 8 | 15 | 20 | Above 20 requires a producer/co-facilitator or split cohort to preserve resume/prompt/interview practice and feedback. |
| In person | 8 | 15–18 | 20 | Above 20 requires a second facilitator or cohort split when hands-on review is required. |

## Low-enrollment rule

SCWDB does not guarantee minimum enrollment for any scheduled session. The proposal should therefore avoid implying that the Board owes payment merely because a session was scheduled below the recommended size.

Proposed operating sequence when enrollment is below 8:

1. notify the authorized SCWDB/LWA contact within the agreed scheduling window;
2. offer consolidation into another live-remote cohort when participant timing and pathway needs allow;
3. offer an approved reschedule;
4. preserve an in-person event when SCWDB determines the Rapid Response or local operational need justifies delivery;
5. apply any cancellation/minimum-session charge only if the executed contract expressly authorizes it.

No participant should be denied service solely because a commercial cohort threshold was not met when SCWDB authorizes delivery under the contract.

## Large Rapid Response events

For plant closures, layoffs, or other concentrated events, Klinikos should not promise unlimited single-room capacity. Instead:

- preserve a common approved curriculum and version;
- add qualified co-facilitators/producers;
- split practice into controlled breakout groups where appropriate;
- use repeated cohorts when interaction or evidence review would otherwise degrade;
- maintain participant-level attendance, assessment, and completion evidence regardless of event size.

The delivery model scales through **staffing and cohort replication**, not by turning the Industry Accelerator into a webinar.

## Instructor staffing assumption for pricing

Until final staffing is verified, internal pricing should model:

- one lead instructor for cohorts within the maximums above;
- additional facilitator/producer cost whenever enrollment exceeds the one-instructor maximum or accommodation complexity requires more support;
- preparation, debrief, evidence review, attendance verification, reporting, and corrective-action time outside live instructional minutes;
- backup-instructor coverage as a program-capacity cost, not an invented participant fee.

The per-completed-participant Appendix B rates must still be able to absorb realistic delivery economics at low utilization. Do not use the 980-person planning target to justify assuming full cohorts.

## Accessibility and digital-skill adjustment

The maximums above are ceilings, not targets. The instructor may recommend a smaller cohort when:

- participants have materially lower baseline digital literacy;
- accessibility/accommodation requirements increase individual facilitation needs;
- the pathway exercise requires more intensive evidence review;
- device/connectivity constraints require more support;
- SCWDB requests a higher-touch Rapid Response format.

A smaller accessible cohort is preferable to meeting a numeric capacity target while degrading meaningful participation.

## Proposal-ready position

> Klinikos recommends highly interactive cohorts rather than webinar-scale classes. For the 6–8 hour Industry Accelerator, our planning target is 12–15 live-remote learners or 12–16 in person, with one-instructor maximums of 18 and 20 respectively. For the 2–3 hour Career Readiness workshop, our planning target is approximately 15 learners, with a one-instructor maximum of 20. Larger events are supported through additional qualified facilitation, controlled breakout groups, or repeated cohorts so hands-on practice, accessibility, and participant-level completion evidence remain intact. The proposed minimum planned cohort is eight, but final low-enrollment, consolidation, cancellation, and Rapid Response rules will follow the executed contract and SCWDB direction.
