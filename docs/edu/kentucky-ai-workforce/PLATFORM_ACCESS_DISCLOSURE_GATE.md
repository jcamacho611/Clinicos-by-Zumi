# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness — Platform Access Disclosure Gate

Status: **internal capture control — proposal disclosure still requires final factual verification.**

Purpose: prevent Klinikos from using its EDU platform as a proposal differentiator while omitting the exact platform disclosures SCWDB says a proprietary technology platform must include.

## Buyer truth locked from the posted Q&A

SCWDB expressly permits a respondent to incorporate a proprietary career-readiness technology platform into the live instructional experience and to provide continued participant access after a workshop.

SCWDB also states that any proposed platform should be clearly described in the proposal, including:

- instructional purpose;
- licensing requirements;
- duration of participant access;
- accessibility features;
- data-retention practices;
- privacy protections; and
- associated costs.

All required participant access should be included in proposed pricing unless otherwise clearly identified. Participants may not be required to purchase software, subscriptions, equipment, or technology as a condition of participation unless SCWDB expressly approves and funds it.

Any platform used must satisfy applicable SCWDB requirements concerning privacy, data security, accessibility, and participant information.

## Proposal rule

Do not describe Klinikos EDU as an included participant platform unless the proposal contains a factual disclosure for every category above.

A missing disclosure is not a cosmetic omission. It creates evaluator uncertainty around cost, access, privacy, accessibility, and post-training obligations.

## 1. Instructional purpose

The proposal may describe Klinikos EDU as an applied practice and evidence layer supporting live instructor-led delivery.

Current intended Kentucky use may include:

- synthetic role-based practice;
- guided responsible-AI exercises;
- participant activities and evidence submission;
- instructor-reviewed assessment evidence;
- attendance/completion support where verified by authorized staff;
- approved curriculum/version context;
- participant-facing materials and continued review access when contractually approved.

Do not represent the software as a substitute for instructors.

Do not represent Zumi/AI as authoritative for attendance, completion, competency, licensure, hiring, clinical decisions, or participant eligibility.

## 2. Licensing requirements

The final proposal must state, in plain language:

- whether SCWDB receives organization-level access, participant seats, cohort/program access, or another license structure;
- whether ordinary required participant access is included in the Appendix B participant price;
- whether instructor/admin access is included;
- whether any third-party licensed component is required for ordinary delivery;
- whether any optional integration or external service could create a separate cost;
- whether access depends on an executed contract, approved program configuration, or other administrative setup.

### Current hard rule

No participant may be required to purchase a Klinikos subscription, AI subscription, device, software license, or external service in order to complete required SCWDB training unless SCWDB expressly approves and funds that requirement.

## 3. Duration of participant access

**Final duration is not yet approved and must not be invented.**

Before proposal submission, choose and document a commercially sustainable access period only after reconciling:

- SCWDB's September 4 addenda;
- Appendix B pricing;
- participant record-retention obligations;
- platform operating cost;
- account lifecycle/security policy;
- post-contract rights;
- whether continued access is instructional reference access or continued active practice access.

The proposal should distinguish at minimum:

1. access during the active workshop/cohort;
2. any continued participant access after completion;
3. access after the contract period ends;
4. administrator/instructor access needed for audit, reporting, or record retention.

Do not write "lifetime access," "permanent access," or an arbitrary number of months without an approved cost and retention model.

## 4. Accessibility features

The proposal may describe only accessibility behavior that has been implemented and verified.

Current proposal disclosures should distinguish:

- implemented accessibility-oriented design and testing;
- accommodations supported operationally by live instructors;
- features still requiring verification;
- formal conformance claims that have **not** been established.

### Prohibited overclaim

Do not claim WCAG 2.2 AA conformance, Section 508 certification, VPAT completion, or another formal accessibility certification unless the required qualified review has actually been completed and the evidence is available.

Before submission, the platform disclosure should identify the specific tested behaviors we can support, such as keyboard navigation, focus visibility, labels, contrast, reduced-motion behavior, responsive/mobile operation, and alternative ways to complete an assessed activity where those behaviors are verified on the exact proposal/demo build.

## 5. Data-retention practices

**Do not invent a retention period.**

The proposal must clearly separate:

- participant account data;
- verified attendance records;
- completion evidence;
- assessment results;
- instructor notes;
- participant feedback;
- employer feedback;
- curriculum/version records;
- audit/security records;
- synthetic practice data.

Before submission, map each category to:

- why it is collected;
- who may access it;
- minimum necessary retention period;
- SCWDB/grant/audit requirement if applicable;
- deletion/archive rule after the applicable period;
- whether the record remains with Klinikos, is exported to SCWDB, or both.

No Kentucky-specific retention promise should be made until the RFP/addenda, grant requirements, executed contract obligations, and Klinikos retention policy are reconciled.

## 6. Privacy protections

The proposal should lead with data minimization and instructional separation rather than generic compliance slogans.

Required disclosure points:

- ordinary EDU scenarios use synthetic training data and should not require real patient PHI;
- participants should be instructed not to enter unnecessary personal, employer-confidential, patient, or regulated information into free-text AI exercises;
- participant records must remain scoped to the authorized institution/program/cohort and role;
- employers do not automatically receive participant submissions, grades, accommodations, attendance, or job-search records;
- outside experts/partners do not gain participant or clinic data merely because support is requested;
- AI-assisted functions remain bounded by the platform's human-authority and disclosure rules;
- logs, exports, reports, and support workflows should expose only the minimum information necessary for the authorized purpose.

Do not claim HIPAA certification or other formal compliance status solely because the system uses privacy controls.

## 7. Data-security disclosure

The final proposal should identify only security controls that are actually implemented and evidenced on the proposed build.

At minimum, verify and document the applicable EDU boundaries for:

- authentication;
- authorization/role gating;
- institution/program/cohort tenant scope;
- synthetic-data separation;
- secure server-side authority for consequential actions;
- auditability of attendance/completion changes;
- prevention of client-side self-asserted completion/authority;
- secret/configuration handling;
- production access and deployment controls;
- incident/escalation ownership.

Do not imply SOC 2, FedRAMP, StateRAMP, HITRUST, HIPAA certification, or penetration-test status unless such evidence exists.

## 8. Associated costs

The disclosure must reconcile with the Appendix B pricing gate.

Default capture rule:

- ordinary platform access required to complete the training is included in the proposed participant economics unless SCWDB permits and approves another structure;
- participants pay $0 directly for required Klinikos EDU access;
- do not create a surprise per-seat fee outside Appendix B;
- optional external integrations or services may be excluded only if clearly identified, genuinely optional, and consistent with the procurement/addenda;
- variable AI, messaging, or external-service costs must not be hidden in a promise of unlimited use if the product cannot sustainably support them.

The final proposal should state exactly what is included and what is not included.

## 9. Post-contract access and IP boundary

This disclosure must be read together with `IP_SCHEDULE.md`.

SCWDB's Q&A distinguishes pre-existing proprietary Klinikos software/curriculum/methodology from materials substantially customized for the Kentucky Network.

Therefore the proposal should state:

- Klinikos retains ownership of pre-existing platform software and reusable methods unless an executed agreement says otherwise;
- SCWDB receives the contractual access/use rights necessary to administer and monitor the approved program;
- Kentucky-specific deliverable ownership/licensing will follow the executed contract;
- continued participant access after completion does not transfer ownership of Klinikos software;
- post-contract administrative access, exports, archival evidence, and transition support must be explicitly defined rather than assumed.

## 10. Exact-head verification gate

Before this disclosure enters the formal proposal, verify the exact proposal/demo build for:

- participant account creation/access;
- role-gated instructor/admin access;
- institution/program/cohort isolation;
- synthetic-only scenario boundaries;
- attendance/completion authority;
- export/report behavior;
- mobile usability;
- keyboard/focus/label behavior;
- no dead links in evaluator routes;
- no invented AI/integration states;
- no migration/deployment claim that exceeds production truth.

If an evaluator-facing platform feature is not live and verified, label it as representative material, planned configuration, manual workflow, or pending connection as appropriate.

## Decision rule

A proprietary platform is a proposal advantage only when the evaluator can answer four questions without guessing:

1. **What does it do for instruction?**
2. **What does it cost and how long can participants use it?**
3. **What participant information does it hold, for how long, and who can access it?**
4. **What accessibility/security/privacy claims are actually evidenced?**

If any answer remains unknown, keep the item open in the capture matrix rather than filling the gap with marketing language.
