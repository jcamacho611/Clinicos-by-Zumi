# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Q&A Capture Q25-Q42

Status: **AUTHORITATIVE BUYER-CLARIFICATION CAPTURE FOR PROPOSAL EXECUTION**

Source: SCWDB published `Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness_RFP Qs & As_2026.08.25.pdf`, Questions 25-42.

This file records buyer clarifications that must govern final proposal packaging, pricing, staffing, technology, IP, credentials, and completion evidence. It does not imply award, eligibility determination, or contract acceptance.

## Q25 — Subcontractor / specialist disclosures at proposal time

SCWDB permits subcontractors, teaming partners, specialist instructors, and SMEs. At proposal submission:

- identify proposed subcontractors in the Proposal Cover Sheet;
- describe relevant subcontractors, specialist instructors, and teaming arrangements in the technical approach, staffing, organizational-capacity, and work-plan sections;
- identify each party's proposed role, responsibilities, qualifications, and relationship to Klinikos;
- no separate subcontractor form has been established beyond the RFP proposal requirements;
- final subcontractor use remains subject to SCWDB review/approval;
- Klinikos remains responsible for performance, quality, compliance, records, reporting, and payment.

**Execution consequence:** any named Gerard/Nadja role used in the external proposal must be consistently classified across Cover Sheet, staffing, work plan, organizational capacity, and budget. Founder-reported willingness is not enough for final signature; scope/availability/compensation treatment must be documented.

## Q26 — Common AI foundation + pathway-specific modules permitted

SCWDB expressly accepts:

`COMMON RESPONSIBLE-AI FOUNDATION + DISTINCT OCCUPATION-SPECIFIC APPLIED MODULES`

The shared foundation may cover responsible AI use, prompting, verification, privacy, cybersecurity, bias awareness, human oversight, and workplace-use boundaries. Each of the five required pathways must still contain meaningful occupation-specific scenarios, exercises, examples, and applications.

**Execution consequence:** preserve the existing Klinikos EDU shared spine; do not create five redundant curriculum systems. Final proposal must prove all five pathway modules are sufficiently distinct and deliverable before participant launch.

## Q27 — No extra prior-contract / entity-age threshold

SCWDB confirms no additional minimum prior-contract value, years-in-business, or workforce-reference requirement beyond the RFP and published clarifications.

**Execution consequence:** do not weaken the proposal by apologizing for Klinikos's recent incorporation. Instead, accurately separate current-prime capability from properly attributed key-person/team experience and current administrative/financial capacity.

## Q28 — Employer / industry validation may occur during implementation

Completed employer validation is not required for all curricula at proposal submission. A documented validation process with SCWDB-designated employers/industry representatives during implementation is acceptable.

**Execution consequence:** include a concrete validation loop and evidence register; do not fabricate completed employer validation or partnerships.

## Q29 / Q34 — Contractor-issued digital credential is acceptable

SCWDB does not require third-party accreditation. A contractor-issued competency-based badge or microcredential is acceptable if the proposal explains:

- represented skills/competencies;
- earning criteria;
- how completion/competency is verified;
- how the credential is documented/shared/verified;
- associated platform/licensing/participant costs.

A certificate of completion remains the minimum requirement.

**Execution consequence:** keep Klinikos EDU credentialing truthful: completion evidence and instructor/human approval create the credential; the credential does not create completion, licensure, accreditation, or professional authority.

## Q30 / Q31 — No guaranteed service or pathway distribution

SCWDB has no expected percentage split across Career Readiness vs Industry Accelerator and no fixed distribution across the five pathways. All five pathways must be available, but delivery frequency may follow actual participant/LWA/employer demand.

**Execution consequence:** pricing and staffing must remain demand-responsive. Never treat the 980 planning target as guaranteed participants, completions, classes, pathway mix, revenue, or payment.

## Q32 — Technology-access assumptions must be explicit

SCWDB says respondents must not assume every participant has:

- an individual computer;
- active email;
- personal AI subscription;
- ability to create a new technology account.

For in-person hosting, SCWDB/LWA locations will generally coordinate a location with standard internet/basic presentation equipment when available, but local technology varies.

The proposal must identify all required devices, internet/bandwidth, AI/software platforms, accounts, licenses/subscriptions, and specialized technology. Required technology/licensing costs should be clearly priced. Participants may not be required to purchase these independently unless approved/funded by SCWDB.

**Execution consequence:** final Appendix B and technical approach must state exactly what Klinikos provides, what host sites provide, what can run without personal AI accounts, and any separately authorized device/license assumption.

## Q33 — Pre-existing Klinikos IP remains separable

SCWDB does not intend to require transfer of ownership of pre-existing proprietary curriculum, materials, frameworks, tools, templates, platforms, methodologies, or other independently developed IP. Final ownership/licensing of Kentucky-specific newly developed or materially customized deliverables will be negotiated.

**Execution consequence:** preserve the pre-existing IP schedule. Do not transfer the Klinikos platform, Zumi architecture, Grid, general EDU curriculum substrate, reusable templates, policy logic, or other background IP through proposal wording. Kentucky-specific deliverables can be licensed/negotiated separately.

## Q35 — Not every future instructor/SME must be named now

The proposal should identify key personnel responsible for contract management, curriculum development, instructional leadership, quality assurance, and other major functions. SCWDB does not require every future instructor or pathway SME to be identified by name at proposal submission. Qualified instructors/SMEs may be added or substituted during implementation subject to contract requirements.

The proposal must explain the staffing model, instructor coverage, recruitment/replacement process, and quality-control mechanism.

**Execution consequence:** do not invent a fully staffed bench. Name only real permissioned key personnel and present a credible, governed coverage/replacement model for the remaining capacity.

## Q36 — Separate pricing components are expressly permitted

SCWDB prefers scalable pricing tied to verified deliverables/completion, but expressly permits separate pricing for:

- one-time curriculum development/customization/implementation/launch;
- per-completed-participant delivery;
- cohort/session pricing;
- in-person delivery;
- approved travel;
- technology/software/licensing/digital-credential costs;
- optional services.

Final payment terms will be negotiated and must comply with federal requirements.

**Execution consequence:** current Appendix B architecture remains responsive. Keep one-time, unit, participant, travel, technology, and optional costs distinct. Do not count unguaranteed volume as booked revenue or cash.

## Q37 — Bidder sets class-size and cancellation economics

SCWDB has no preferred cancellation window or required minimum participant threshold. Respondents should state their own recommended minimum/preferred/maximum class sizes, minimum-enrollment/session-charge assumptions, cancellation/rescheduling rules, and low-enrollment pricing conditions.

SCWDB encourages reasonable and flexible terms and does not guarantee minimum enrollment.

**Execution consequence:** preserve the current low-volume/cancellation protections unless later addenda supersede them; stress-test them against instructor/subcontractor costs and delayed payment.

## Q38 / Q39 — Teaming may satisfy in-person Kentucky delivery; no office required

SCWDB permits an out-of-state prime and explicitly allows subcontractors/teaming partners to support required Kentucky in-person delivery while the prime provides curriculum, live remote instruction, responsible-AI content, program management, QA, reporting, and other functions.

No existing Kentucky office/facility is required at proposal submission.

**Execution consequence:** do not create or pay for a Kentucky office merely for eligibility. Prove credible in-person deployment through actual personnel/teaming/travel coverage.

## Q40 — Bidder chooses reasonable cohort assumptions

No standard average cohort size or service distribution is supplied. Respondents must use and disclose their own reasonable assumptions.

**Execution consequence:** keep all pricing scenarios visibly assumption-based, not forecasts.

## Q41 — In-person travel may be separately invoiced

SCWDB expressly says occasional in-person travel/lodging need not be included in per-completed-participant rates. Respondents may separately identify in-person delivery and approved travel, provided the proposal clearly distinguishes included costs, separately billed costs, and travel assumptions/limits.

**Execution consequence:** preserve separate travel treatment. Never bury uncertain statewide travel into the participant rate unless a later negotiated contract requires it.

## Q42 — Completion standard will be approved during implementation

SCWDB will approve a uniform completion standard for each workshop during implementation/curriculum approval. Respondents should recommend criteria and documentation. Final completion may include attendance, required activities, assessments/demonstrations, and other approved criteria.

Registration or partial attendance alone does not qualify as completion for payment.

**Execution consequence:** Klinikos's deterministic completion-review gate is aligned with buyer intent. Invoice only from buyer-approved completion evidence; login, registration, invitation, partial attendance, or certificate issuance alone are not payment truth.

1. Proposed subcontractors/key specialists are disclosed consistently everywhere required.
2. Every named person's status is one of: `COMMITTED/EVIDENCED`, `PROPOSED/FORMALIZATION PENDING`, or `VALIDATION ONLY`.
3. Common-AI foundation remains paired with five genuinely distinct occupation-specific modules.
4. No apology or false corporate-history claim is used; prime capacity and key-person history stay separate.
5. Employer validation is presented as a formal implementation process unless existing evidence is actually available.
6. Credential language never implies accreditation/licensure not held.
7. Pricing assumes no guaranteed 980 participants, pathway mix, class count, enrollment minimum, or payment.
8. Technology requirements and participant-access costs are explicitly disclosed.
9. Background Klinikos IP is scheduled and protected.
10. Staffing/replacement/coverage is credible without inventing a full named bench.
11. One-time, per-completion, session, in-person, travel, technology, credential, and optional costs remain distinguishable.
12. Low-volume/cancellation terms remain flexible but financially viable.
13. Kentucky physical-office costs are not introduced without an actual business reason.
14. Travel remains separately controlled where appropriate.
15. Completion invoicing is tied only to SCWDB-approved verified completion evidence.
16. September 4 addenda and any later buyer clarification supersede this capture where inconsistent.
