## 5. Liquidity test

The model's intended decision chain is:

`ACTUAL COST INPUTS → DELIVERY COST → CASH-CYCLE COST → REQUIRED RESERVE → ESTIMATED WORKING CAPITAL`

then:

`ACTUAL UNRESTRICTED CASH + ACTUALLY APPROVED USABLE CREDIT = VERIFIED AVAILABLE LIQUIDITY`

then:

`VERIFIED AVAILABLE LIQUIDITY ÷ ESTIMATED WORKING CAPITAL = COVERAGE`

Internal planning interpretation:

- `>= 1.20x` → working-capital coverage passes the current internal buffer test;
- `1.00x–1.19x` → thin, requires management review and likely additional buffer;
- `< 1.00x` → financing gap;
- missing required input → `INPUTS REQUIRED`.

The `1.20x` threshold is an internal risk-control assumption, not an SCWDB requirement.

## 6. What may count as available liquidity

Only amounts that are actually accessible for contract performance, such as:

- unrestricted Klinikos business cash supported by private bank/accounting evidence;
- a closed/approved and currently usable business credit facility;
- a documented owner contribution actually funded or irrevocably available on acceptable terms;
- other committed company funding that can legally and practically be used for contract performance.

## 7. What must NOT count as available liquidity

Do not count:

- a submitted loan application;
- a lender conversation;
- an unaccepted term sheet;
- a credit-card preapproval/marketing offer;
- grant applications;
- accelerator applications;
- prize maximums;
- customer pipeline;
- unsigned proposals;
- Stripe payment links without completed payment evidence;
- the SCWDB opportunity itself;
- expected invoice revenue that has not yet become a valid receivable under an executed contract;
- the approximately 980-participant planning figure.

`POSSIBLE MONEY ≠ AVAILABLE LIQUIDITY`
