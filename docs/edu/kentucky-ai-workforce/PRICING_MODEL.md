# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Pricing Scenario Model

Status: **internal capture and finance model**. This is not a submitted quote, SCWDB-approved price, guaranteed award value, or representation of guaranteed participant volume.

## Current procurement anchor

SCWDB's currently posted Q&A states that the approximately 980 unique participants are a **planning target, not a guaranteed minimum**, and that Appendix B requests **per-completed-participant pricing**. Completion must be supported by verified attendance and other approved completion evidence; registration or partial attendance alone does not establish a payable completion.

Accordingly, Klinikos must maintain two distinct pricing views:

1. **Customer-facing Appendix B:** responsive per-completed-participant rates and any other line items expressly requested/allowed by the RFP or addenda.
2. **Internal unit-economics model:** the fixed, cohort, instructor, participant, platform, travel, working-capital, QA, reporting, and subcontractor costs that must be recovered by those customer-facing rates.

Do not submit an internally convenient blended pricing structure if it conflicts with Appendix B.

## Pricing rules

1. Never model `980 × rate` as guaranteed revenue.
2. Do not assume a startup-style deposit unless SCWDB expressly permits it.
3. Separate fixed implementation cost from variable delivery cost internally, even when the customer-facing rate must be per completion.
4. Identify recommended, minimum, and maximum class sizes for live-remote and in-person delivery.
5. Keep travel, minimum enrollment, cancellation, and rescheduling assumptions explicit.
6. Do not price unbuilt capabilities as if production-ready, including automated instruction, automated competency certification, unsupported institutional integrations, or completed specialized simulators that do not exist.
7. Required participant platform access must be included in proposed pricing unless SCWDB expressly permits another treatment.
8. Use integer-dollar customer schedules and retain a documented internal cost basis beneath every proposed amount.
9. Do not represent a financial-capacity source unless the evidence actually exists.

## Internal cost architecture

### Fixed implementation and launch

Model at least:
- kickoff and discovery;
- final curriculum/objective mapping;
- all five pathway configuration and finalization;
- Career Readiness configuration;
- representative and participant materials;
- instructor-guide finalization;
- accessibility review/remediation before first delivery;
- completion/reporting configuration;
- instructor calibration and backup coverage;
- launch QA and program-management setup;
- work required to meet the 30-day launch window after notice to proceed.

### Live delivery

Model separately for:
- Service A live-remote cohort;
- Service A in-person cohort;
- Service B live-remote workshop;
- Service B in-person workshop;
- instructor/SME preparation;
- attendance/completion administration;
- assessment and human review;
- participant support;
- reporting and quality assurance;
- rescheduling/cancellation exposure.

### Participant-dependent cost

Use for genuine participant-driven cost only:
- approved participant materials;
- certificate/completion evidence;
- bounded platform access;
- participant-level assessment/reporting administration;
- legitimately funded variable-cost tools.

### Travel and distributed delivery

Track separately internally:
- instructor travel time;
- mileage/airfare;
- lodging;
- permitted meals/per diem;
- local transportation;
- backup instructor coverage caused by travel;
- short-notice Rapid Response deployment exposure.

Do not promise unlimited statewide in-person delivery inside a rate that cannot sustain the travel assumptions.

## Required completion-rate scenarios

For each Appendix B rate, calculate economics under at least these utilization states.

### Low utilization

Stress case: approximately 25% of the planning volume, with uneven pathway demand.

Questions:
- Can fixed implementation cost still be recovered?
- What minimum viable cohort economics are required?
- What happens if in-person travel is disproportionately high?
- What is the gross margin if participant completions arrive slowly?

### Expected utilization

Use the 980-person figure only as a planning scenario, never a commitment.

Model:
- Service A only completions;
- Service B only completions;
- participants completing both where permitted;
- completion mix across all five pathways;
- remote versus in-person mix;
- average cohort size;
- instructor bench and reviewer load.

### High utilization

Model:
- concurrent cohorts;
- instructor/SME bench capacity;
- reporting/QA capacity;
- travel conflicts;
- feedback and curriculum-review load;
- any volume tiers only where marginal cost genuinely falls.

## Appendix B conversion model

For each customer-facing per-completed-participant rate:

`Required completion rate = (allocated fixed cost + expected delivery cost + participant variable cost + expected travel exposure + subcontractor cost + risk reserve) / expected payable completions`

Then stress that rate against low, expected, and high completion volumes.

A proposed rate is not acceptable merely because it works at the 980-person scenario. It must remain commercially survivable under materially lower utilization without relying on unsupported customer minimums.

## Margin model

`Gross margin = recognized contract revenue - direct delivery cost - participant variable cost - unreimbursed travel - delivery-specific subcontractor cost`

Maintain a separate contribution-margin view for corporate overhead and founder/management compensation rather than hiding those costs inside instruction.

Minimum internal checks:
- low-utilization gross margin;
- expected-utilization gross margin;
- high-utilization staffing capacity;
- pathway-mix sensitivity;
- travel sensitivity;
- cancellation/no-show sensitivity;
- completion-rate sensitivity;
- instructor/subcontractor economics;
- 30-day launch cost;
- reporting/QA burden;
- platform and approved variable-cost usage.

## Working-capital stress test

For each pricing scenario model collection at:
- 30 days after accepted invoice;
- 45 days;
- 60 days;
- 90 days.

`Peak working capital = unpaid direct labor + unpaid subcontractor obligations + unreimbursed travel + participant/material cost + implementation cost incurred before acceptance + required program administration`

Do not count expected contract revenue as cash before receipt.

Before final pricing approval document:
1. who finances instructors/subcontractors before payment;
2. instructor/subcontractor payment timing;
3. travel booking and reimbursement exposure;
4. maximum concurrently financeable cohorts;
5. pre-revenue launch/configuration burn;
6. the **verified** working-capital source;
7. contingency for delayed SCWDB payment.

No proposal may claim an unverified cash balance, credit facility, investor, grant, funding commitment, or partner contribution.

## Qualification and teaming economics

The currently posted Q&A states that the respondent organization must demonstrate at least three years of relevant experience and permits subcontractors/teaming partners while keeping the prime accountable. Any teaming solution used to satisfy delivery specialization or qualification strategy must therefore be costed into:
- completion-rate economics;
- working-capital requirements;
- subcontractor payment timing;
- QA/reporting overhead;
- insurance/compliance administration.

Do not treat partner labor as free or imply a partner relationship that is not documented.

## Final pricing gate

Before proposal submission:
- reconcile every rate and line item to the latest RFP, posted Q&A, and September 4 addenda;
- confirm Appendix B format exactly;
- document class-size/travel/minimum-enrollment/cancellation assumptions;
- confirm participant-access cost treatment;
- confirm completion evidence used for invoicing;
- stress-test rates against low/expected/high utilization and 30/45/60/90-day cash collection;
- obtain internal approval that the price is commercially viable without guaranteed volume;
- never expose unsupported internal financial assumptions as established facts.

## Proposal posture

Klinikos should present predictable per-completed-participant pricing that is responsive to Appendix B while explaining that delivery is engineered for variable demand, verified completion, remote-first statewide reach, strategic in-person deployment, and measurable reporting. Internally, every customer-facing rate must recover the real implementation and delivery economics without assuming the planning target is guaranteed.
