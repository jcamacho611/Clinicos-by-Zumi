# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Skeptical Evaluator Scorecard

Status: **capture working document**. This scorecard is an internal proposal-quality gate. It does not imply SCWDB approval, eligibility, award, scoring preference, or contract terms beyond the published RFP/Q&A.

## Purpose

Before the September 18, 2026 proposal is signed and submitted, evaluate the response as if we are a skeptical SCWDB evaluator comparing Klinikos against older workforce-training organizations.

The proposal should not rely on startup enthusiasm, code volume, or product novelty to compensate for missing procurement evidence. Every scored strength must point to a proposal section, representative artifact, product proof, named person, or verifiable record.

## Published evaluation themes

SCWDB's posted Q&A states that proposals will be evaluated using Section 8 criteria including:

- understanding of the Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network;
- technical approach;
- curriculum quality;
- occupational pathway design;
- implementation capacity; and
- cost effectiveness.

The same Q&A states that contractor performance is expected to emphasize participant satisfaction and skill gain, quality/relevance of instruction, instructor effectiveness, scheduling reliability, accessibility and engagement, timely/accurate reporting, continuous curriculum improvement based on participant/employer feedback, and compliance with data/privacy/documentation/grant requirements.

This internal scorecard therefore tests both proposal awardability and whether the operating model can credibly perform after award.

## Internal rating scale

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Missing, unsupported, contradictory, or nonresponsive |
| 1 | Mentioned but weak, generic, unproven, or materially incomplete |
| 2 | Responsive and supportable, but not differentiated or not fully evidenced |
| 3 | Strong, specific, evidence-linked, and evaluator-friendly |
| 4 | Exceptional, demonstrable, low-risk, and clearly differentiated without overclaiming |

A proposal should not be considered submission-ready if any pass/fail gate is red, even if the narrative score is high.

## Pass/fail red gates

Do **not** recommend final submission as a standalone Klinikos prime while any of the following is unresolved:

1. Three-year relevant-experience requirement cannot be supported under SCWDB's controlling interpretation.
2. Three comparable references are not verifiable and proposal-appropriate.
3. Named instructional capacity cannot credibly support live remote plus responsive in-person delivery.
4. Administrative/financial capacity is unsupported for launch and reimbursement timing.
5. Appendix B pricing is nonresponsive to the final RFP/addenda format.
6. Required certifications, representations, registrations, disclosures, or signature authority are incomplete.

A red gate is a bidder-structure problem, not a copywriting problem. Do not attempt to solve it with stronger adjectives.

## Win themes to preserve

### 1. Applied AI, not another slideshow

Klinikos combines live instruction with role-based practice, evidence, verification, and human feedback. The software is the practice/delivery layer; the instructor remains the educational authority.

### 2. Review before action

The learner is trained to recognize where AI is useful, minimize sensitive disclosure, detect unreliable output, verify independently, respect employer/safety boundaries, escalate when required, and document the final human-owned action.

### 3. Healthcare proof, reusable cross-industry method

Healthcare is the strongest existing simulation foundation. Manufacturing, construction, logistics, and business operations use the same responsible-AI instructional architecture with occupation-specific scenarios and boundaries. Do not imply specialized production simulators where none exist.

### 4. Evidence instead of attendance theater

Enrollment and login do not equal attendance; attendance does not equal completion; completion does not equal licensure or professional competency. The program preserves explicit evidence and human review for each state transition.

### 5. Truthful technology

Klinikos should gain evaluator trust by naming what is built, what is proposal configuration, what is pending connection, and what requires implementation approval. Avoid turning ambitious product direction into procurement claims.

## Final red-team questions

Before signature, have a reviewer unfamiliar with the build answer these from the proposal alone:

- Why is Klinikos more likely to create usable workplace behavior than a conventional AI workshop vendor?
- What exactly happens during a participant's first hour, practical exercise, assessment, and completion review?
- How will the organization serve a participant in a rural Kentucky region next month if the preferred instructor is unavailable?
- What evidence proves each proposed instructor can teach adults rather than merely use AI?
- What happens if only 40 participants complete in a quarter instead of the modeled volume?
- What records prove a completion for invoicing and audit?
- Which participant data does Klinikos retain, for how long, and who can see it?
- Which platform capabilities are actually live today, which are branch/proposal capabilities, and which are planned?
- What part of the curriculum/software remains Klinikos IP, and what can SCWDB continue using under the final agreement?
- Why should an evaluator trust a newly formed company to perform a multi-year workforce contract?

If the proposal cannot answer the last question with documented people, systems, references, financial/administrative capacity, and demonstrated product evidence, the bid is not ready regardless of design quality.
