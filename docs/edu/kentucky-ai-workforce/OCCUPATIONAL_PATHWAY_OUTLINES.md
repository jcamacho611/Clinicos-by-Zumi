# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Occupational Pathway Outlines and Sample Lesson Segments

Status: **proposal/capture working document**. These are representative, buyer-specific pathway outlines and lesson segments prepared to satisfy SCWDB proposal requirements. They are not SCWDB-approved curricula, not evidence that all five production curricula are already implemented in Klinikos EDU, and not regulated professional training or licensure content.

## Purpose and truth boundary

SCWDB's posted Q&A requires a responsive proposal to address all five AI Industry Accelerator pathways and provide an outline and sample lesson segment for each pathway. The existing Klinikos EDU healthcare simulation is the strongest built domain foundation. Manufacturing, construction, logistics, and business operations are represented here as **proposal-time instructional designs using the same reusable responsible-AI methodology**; full production curriculum, pathway-specific instructor materials, assessments, and institutional configuration would be finalized during implementation and approved before participant delivery.

Across every pathway, the common instructional spine is:

`Understand the task → decide whether AI is appropriate → minimize sensitive input → prompt clearly → inspect output → verify material facts → apply employer policy → escalate when authority is required → document the final human-owned action`

AI is treated as an assistive tool, not as final authority.

## 1. Manufacturing

### Proposed pathway objective

Help manufacturing workers use AI responsibly for non-safety-critical operational tasks such as work-instruction drafting, shift communication, inventory organization, quality-document review, training support, and structured problem solving while preserving employer procedures, safety controls, proprietary information, and human authorization.

### Proposed pathway outline

1. AI fundamentals in modern manufacturing workplaces
2. Appropriate versus inappropriate AI-assisted tasks
3. Protecting proprietary process information and credentials
4. Prompting for structured work instructions and shift handoffs
5. Reviewing AI-generated checklists, summaries, and troubleshooting ideas
6. Fact-checking against approved SOPs, specifications, and maintenance documentation
7. Safety-critical boundaries and supervisor escalation
8. Quality documentation and change-control discipline
9. Applied manufacturing scenario
10. Human-reviewed assessment and completion evidence

### Sample lesson segment — "Draft, Verify, Then Release"

**Scenario:** A production associate receives a synthetic shift-handoff note describing a recurring packaging-line stoppage. An AI draft suggests a revised restart checklist and states that one sensor tolerance should be adjusted.

**Participant task:**

- identify which parts of the AI draft are ordinary communication/work-instruction assistance;
- flag the sensor-tolerance change as requiring authoritative technical documentation and authorized personnel;
- compare the AI draft with a provided synthetic approved SOP excerpt;
- remove unsupported steps;
- rewrite the handoff using only verified facts;
- identify what proprietary or credential information should not be entered into an unapproved AI service;
- state who must approve any process or parameter change.

**Evidence submitted:** corrected shift handoff, verification note, escalation decision, data-minimization note.

**Human authority boundary:** AI cannot authorize equipment settings, bypass lockout/tagout, change safety procedures, or approve a process deviation.

### Sample assessment prompt

An AI response recommends skipping a documented inspection step because recent batches "appear stable." What should the worker do next, and why?

**Expected principle:** do not use AI output to bypass approved quality or safety procedures; verify against authoritative documentation and escalate through the required human process.

## 2. Construction

### Proposed pathway objective

Help construction workers and supervisors use AI for bounded administrative and planning support such as meeting notes, task sequencing drafts, material organization, communication, document summaries, and training reinforcement while preserving site safety, code requirements, contractual controls, confidential project information, and authorized professional judgment.

### Proposed pathway outline

1. AI fundamentals for construction operations
2. Safe prompting with project and client information
3. Using AI to organize daily plans, meeting notes, and RFIs
4. Reviewing AI-generated task sequences and material summaries
5. Verification against drawings, specifications, codes, and approved instructions
6. Recognizing licensed-professional and safety-authority boundaries
7. Cybersecurity, phishing, and document-upload risks
8. Change-order and documentation integrity
9. Applied construction scenario
10. Instructor-reviewed assessment

### Sample lesson segment — "Plan Support Is Not Site Authority"

**Scenario:** A synthetic superintendent note asks AI to organize tomorrow's work around a concrete pour, delivery windows, and crew sequencing. The AI draft invents a curing-time assumption and recommends moving a temporary barrier to improve equipment access.

**Participant task:**

- identify the invented curing-time assumption;
- identify the barrier change as a safety/site-authority issue;
- verify scheduling facts against the provided synthetic plan and approved instruction;
- produce a corrected coordination note;
- identify project information that should not be uploaded to an unapproved AI tool;
- name the responsible role for approving the barrier or sequence change.

**Evidence submitted:** corrected daily coordination plan, verification sources, escalation note, privacy/confidentiality rationale.

**Human authority boundary:** AI cannot approve engineering decisions, code interpretations, safety-plan changes, permits, inspections, or professional sign-off.

### Sample assessment prompt

An AI summary states that a drawing revision "probably does not affect structural work." How should a participant handle the statement?

**Expected principle:** treat the statement as unverified; check the authoritative drawing/revision process and route the question to the authorized professional or supervisor.

## 3. Logistics

### Proposed pathway objective

Help logistics workers use AI for communication, shipment-information organization, exception triage, scheduling support, customer-service drafting, and process analysis while protecting customer/vendor data, shipment security, regulatory requirements, and human dispatch/operations authority.

### Proposed pathway outline

1. AI fundamentals in logistics and supply-chain work
2. Appropriate use for scheduling, communication, and information organization
3. Minimum-disclosure prompting with shipment/customer information
4. AI-assisted exception summaries and handoffs
5. Verification against TMS/WMS records and authoritative shipment data
6. Fraud, phishing, credential, and document risks
7. Regulatory and hazardous-material boundaries
8. Dispatch and escalation authority
9. Applied logistics scenario
10. Human-reviewed performance evidence

### Sample lesson segment — "Exception Triage Without Inventing the Shipment"

**Scenario:** A synthetic shipment is delayed. The provided record contains an updated carrier ETA, a customer delivery window, and a warehouse receiving cutoff. An AI-generated response invents a new delivery commitment and recommends rerouting the load through a location not present in the approved record.

**Participant task:**

- identify the invented commitment and unverified reroute;
- verify the actual ETA and receiving cutoff;
- draft a truthful customer/operations update;
- identify which data should be withheld from an unapproved AI tool;
- state which role may authorize a reroute or commercial commitment;
- document the final escalation/action.

**Evidence submitted:** corrected update, verification log, authority decision, data-minimization note.

**Human authority boundary:** AI cannot independently commit delivery terms, alter regulated routing, authorize hazardous-material handling, or override dispatch/security rules.

### Sample assessment prompt

An AI draft turns an estimated ETA into a guaranteed delivery time. What is the risk and the correct response?

**Expected principle:** distinguish estimates from commitments, verify the source data, and obtain human authorization before making a commercial promise.

## 4. Healthcare

### Proposed pathway objective

Teach responsible AI literacy for administrative and operational healthcare work such as scheduling, communication, documentation support, information organization, referral/follow-up coordination, and workflow support while protecting privacy and preserving licensed clinical and organizational authority.

### Existing foundation

Healthcare is the pathway with the strongest current Klinikos EDU product foundation: the synthetic Virtual Clinic Lab, eight simulation roles, healthcare curriculum packages, instructor-authoritative grading, and structural separation from real clinical patient records.

### Proposed pathway outline

1. What generative AI can and cannot reliably do
2. Safe prompting and minimum disclosure
3. Privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, and employer policy
4. Scheduling, communication, and information-organization support
5. Documentation support without fabricated facts
6. Fact-checking, source verification, and hallucination detection
7. Human authority, role boundaries, and escalation
8. Virtual Clinic Lab applied scenario
9. Instructor review and debrief
10. Career-readiness translation

### Sample lesson segment — "Review Before Action"

**Scenario:** A synthetic patient workflow shows an overdue referral, incomplete paperwork, and a scheduling conflict. An AI-generated administrative summary contains one unsupported factual claim and one recommendation that exceeds the learner's assigned role.

**Participant task:**

- identify the unsupported claim;
- identify the role-boundary violation;
- verify the source facts;
- correct the administrative summary;
- explain what must be escalated and to whom;
- identify information that should not be entered into an unapproved external AI system;
- submit the corrected operational work for instructor review.

**Evidence submitted:** unsupported-claim identification, verification note, corrected draft, escalation decision, data-minimization note.

**Human authority boundary:** AI cannot diagnose, prescribe, authorize care, determine clinical scope, certify competence, or replace licensed judgment.

### Sample assessment prompt

An AI draft states that a learner is "HIPAA certified" because they completed a privacy lesson. Correct the statement.

**Expected principle:** course completion must not be represented as professional certification, licensure, accreditation, or scope of practice.

## 5. Business Operations

### Proposed pathway objective

Help participants use AI responsibly for general business workflows such as communication, meeting summaries, process documentation, customer support drafts, spreadsheet interpretation, project planning, policy review, and administrative productivity while protecting confidential information, financial truth, access controls, and managerial authority.

### Proposed pathway outline

1. AI fundamentals for office and business operations
2. Selecting tasks appropriate for AI assistance
3. Prompting without exposing confidential business information
4. Drafting and summarizing communications and procedures
5. Reviewing AI-generated spreadsheet/business analysis
6. Verification of numbers, policies, dates, and commitments
7. Cybersecurity, credential, and social-engineering risks
8. Approval authority for finance, HR, legal, and contractual actions
9. Applied business-operations scenario
10. Instructor-reviewed assessment and career transfer

### Sample lesson segment — "Useful Draft, Real Numbers"

**Scenario:** A synthetic operations coordinator uses AI to summarize a monthly service report and draft a vendor follow-up. The AI response invents a 12% cost saving, misstates a contract renewal date, and recommends telling the vendor that an extension has been approved.

**Participant task:**

- identify the fabricated metric, incorrect date, and unauthorized commitment;
- verify figures against the provided synthetic source table and contract excerpt;
- rewrite the report and vendor message using only supported facts;
- identify what confidential financial/vendor data should not be shared with an unapproved AI tool;
- name the person/role authorized to approve contract changes or financial claims.

**Evidence submitted:** corrected summary, verification table, corrected vendor message, escalation/approval note.

**Human authority boundary:** AI cannot approve contracts, make financial commitments, invent KPIs, make employment decisions, or act as legal/HR/finance authority.

### Sample assessment prompt

AI produces a polished executive summary containing a percentage improvement not present in the source data. What should the participant do?

**Expected principle:** remove or independently calculate/verify the claim from authoritative data before use; polished language does not make a number true.

## Cross-pathway scoring framework

Each sample can use the same instructor-controlled 0–2 evidence scale:

- **0 — Not demonstrated:** materially unsafe, unsupported, fabricated, or omitted.
- **1 — Developing:** partially correct but incomplete or requires correction.
- **2 — Demonstrated:** accurate, appropriately bounded, independently verifiable, and explainable by the participant.

Recommended critical domains across all pathways:

1. Privacy / Minimum Disclosure
2. Verification / Fact-Checking
3. Human Authority / Escalation
4. Truthful Documentation / Communication

A participant should not be treated as successfully completing an applied exercise while scoring `0` in a critical domain. Final completion standards remain subject to SCWDB approval.

## Instructor delivery model

The same delivery pattern supports every pathway while allowing qualified pathway specialists to vary examples and domain sources:

1. Instructor introduces the workplace task and AI boundary.
2. Instructor models safe prompting and verification.
3. Participant receives a realistic synthetic scenario and flawed/partial AI output.
4. Participant checks source material, corrects the output, and identifies authority boundaries.
5. Instructor reviews evidence using the common rubric plus pathway-specific criteria.
6. Group debrief connects the scenario to employer policy and real workplace practice.

This preserves a reusable institutional Klinikos EDU model while avoiding the false claim that the four non-healthcare production curricula already exist.
