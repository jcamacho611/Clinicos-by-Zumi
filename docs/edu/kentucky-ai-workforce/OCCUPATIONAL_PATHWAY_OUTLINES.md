# SCWDB AI Industry Accelerator — Occupational Pathway Outlines

Status: **proposed buyer-specific instructional design, not SCWDB-approved curriculum.**

All five pathways use the same responsible-AI instructional spine:

`Understand the task → decide whether AI is appropriate → minimize sensitive input → prompt clearly → inspect output → verify consequential facts → apply employer policy → escalate when authority is required → document the final human-owned action`

Each pathway is designed for 6–8 hours of live instructor-led delivery with hands-on practice. The software representation is canonical in `src/lib/edu/kentucky-ai-workforce.ts`; this document is the proposal-friendly narrative view.

## Manufacturing

### Purpose

Help manufacturing workers use AI for bounded operational tasks such as production documentation, shift handoffs, approved work-instruction drafting, quality-document review, inventory organization, and structured troubleshooting while preserving safety procedures, proprietary information, approved technical specifications, and human authorization.

### Outline

1. AI fundamentals and limitations
2. Appropriate manufacturing use cases
3. Protecting proprietary and restricted information
4. Prompting for work instructions and communication
5. Reviewing checklists, summaries, and troubleshooting drafts
6. Verifying against SOPs/specifications and approved sources
7. Safety-critical and maintenance authority boundaries
8. Applied exercise: **Draft, Verify, Then Release**
9. Instructor rubric/debrief
10. Completion evidence and take-away resource

### Sample segment

A synthetic AI draft improves a shift handoff but recommends changing a sensor tolerance not supported by the supplied approved work instruction. The learner must identify the unsupported change, verify the source, produce a corrected handoff, minimize sensitive input, and identify who has authority to approve a parameter change.

## Construction

### Purpose

Help construction workers use AI for project communication, meeting notes, field documentation, material/schedule coordination, estimating support, and process organization while protecting site safety, drawings/specifications, code and permit processes, confidential project information, and licensed-professional authority.

### Outline

1. AI fundamentals for construction operations
2. Safe prompting with project information
3. Daily plans, notes, RFIs, and coordination drafts
4. Reviewing AI-generated sequencing/material summaries
5. Verification against drawings, specifications, codes, and approved instructions
6. Cybersecurity and document-upload risks
7. Safety, engineering, permit, and contractual authority boundaries
8. Applied exercise: **Plan Support Is Not Site Authority**
9. Instructor rubric/debrief
10. Completion evidence and take-away resource

### Sample segment

An AI draft for tomorrow's concrete-pour coordination invents a curing-time assumption and recommends moving a temporary barrier. The learner must verify the schedule, flag the unsafe/unauthorized recommendation, correct the coordination note, and identify the responsible human authority.

## Logistics

### Purpose

Help logistics workers use AI for inventory review, routing/scheduling support, shipment communication, warehouse documentation, supply-chain analysis, and exception handling while preserving shipment security, customer/vendor confidentiality, authoritative records, regulatory requirements, and dispatch/commercial authority.

### Outline

1. AI fundamentals in logistics
2. Scheduling, communication, and information organization
3. Minimum-disclosure prompting with shipment/customer data
4. Exception summaries and handoffs
5. Verification against TMS/WMS or supplied system-of-record data
6. Fraud, phishing, credential, and document risks
7. Routing, hazardous-material, dispatch, and commitment authority
8. Applied exercise: **Exception Triage Without Inventing the Shipment**
9. Instructor rubric/debrief
10. Completion evidence and take-away resource

### Sample segment

A delayed-shipment AI draft converts an estimated ETA into a guaranteed commitment and suggests an unapproved reroute. The learner must identify both errors, verify the real data, create a truthful update, protect confidential information, and escalate the reroute/commitment decision.

## Healthcare

### Purpose

Teach responsible AI literacy for **nonclinical and administrative healthcare work**: communication, scheduling, documentation support, information organization, referral/follow-up coordination, privacy awareness, verification, employer policy, and appropriate limits on AI use.

Healthcare is the pathway with the strongest existing Klinikos EDU foundation because the Virtual Clinic Lab already supports synthetic healthcare roles and instructor-reviewable practice.

### Outline

1. AI capabilities and limitations
2. Safe prompting and minimum disclosure
3. Privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, and employer policy
4. Scheduling, communication, and information-organization support
5. Documentation support without fabricated facts
6. Fact-checking and hallucination/error detection
7. Role boundaries, escalation, and human clinical authority
8. Applied Virtual Clinic Lab exercise: **Review Before Action**
9. Instructor rubric/debrief
10. Career-readiness translation and completion evidence

### Sample segment

A synthetic patient workflow contains an overdue referral, missing paperwork, and scheduling conflict. An AI administrative summary introduces one unsupported claim and one recommendation outside the learner's role. The learner verifies the source, corrects the draft, identifies what to escalate, and documents what data should not be sent to an unapproved AI tool.

**Boundary:** the pathway does not train AI diagnosis, prescribing, treatment selection, independent clinical judgment, scope-of-practice determination, or care authorization.

## Business Operations

### Purpose

Help participants use AI responsibly for office productivity, customer/professional communication, meetings/reports, scheduling, process documentation, data review, and decision-support drafts while protecting confidential business information and preserving finance, HR, legal, contract, access-control, and executive authority.

### Outline

1. AI fundamentals for office/business work
2. Selecting tasks appropriate for AI assistance
3. Prompting without exposing confidential data
4. Communication, meeting, report, and process drafts
5. Reviewing AI-generated analysis and spreadsheet interpretations
6. Verifying numbers, dates, policies, and commitments
7. Cybersecurity, credential, and social-engineering risks
8. Applied exercise: **Useful Draft, Real Numbers**
9. Instructor rubric/debrief
10. Completion evidence and take-away resource

### Sample segment

An AI summary invents a 12% cost saving, misstates a renewal date, and tells a vendor an extension is approved. The learner must verify the underlying data, correct the output, protect confidential information, and identify who can approve a contract or financial commitment.

## Common completion evidence

Each pathway should use an SCWDB-approved variation of:

- verified attendance;
- participation in required hands-on activity;
- completion of required knowledge/verification checks;
- participant evidence demonstrating source verification and human-accountability decisions;
- instructor review and approval;
- certificate/completion record with material/version provenance.

Attendance alone is not treated as successful completion.
