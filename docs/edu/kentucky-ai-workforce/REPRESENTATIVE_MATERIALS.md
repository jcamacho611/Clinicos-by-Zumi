# Klinikos EDU — Representative SCWDB Workforce Training Materials

Status: **proposed representative materials for proposal/demo use. Not SCWDB-approved curriculum.**

These materials are the canonical proposal examples for the Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network. They demonstrate live instructor-led delivery, applied practice, verification, privacy/security, human accountability, and instructor-controlled completion.

## Sample slide outline — Responsible AI at Work

1. What generative AI is and is not
2. Where AI can save time
3. Where confident AI can still be wrong
4. Safe prompting and minimum disclosure
5. Privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, intellectual property, and employer policy
6. Fact-checking, source verification, bias awareness, and error detection
7. Human authority and escalation
8. Occupational pathway example
9. Hands-on activity with intentionally flawed AI output
10. Review, correction, evidence, and take-away checklist

### Instructor message

AI can help workers draft, organize, compare, summarize, and practice. It does not transfer accountability away from the worker or employer. Consequential output must be checked against an authoritative source or accountable person before action.

## Participant activity — Review Before Action

**Pathway:** Healthcare  
**Role:** Medical Assistant  
**Environment:** Synthetic Virtual Clinic Lab  
**Recommended activity time:** 25–35 minutes

### Scenario

A synthetic patient workflow contains an overdue referral, incomplete intake paperwork, and a scheduling conflict. An AI-generated administrative summary is provided. The summary contains one unsupported factual claim and one recommendation that exceeds the learner's assigned role.

### Learner tasks

1. Identify the unsupported claim.
2. Identify which source fact fails to support or contradicts it.
3. Identify the recommendation outside the assigned role.
4. Name the authorized role to whom the issue should be escalated.
5. Rewrite the administrative summary using supported facts only.
6. Identify information that should not be placed into an unapproved external AI service.
7. Submit the corrected work for instructor review.

### Required evidence

- unsupported-claim identification;
- verification note;
- authority/escalation decision;
- corrected operational draft;
- minimum-disclosure/privacy note.

No real patient information is required or permitted for the ordinary exercise.

## Sample assessment items

### Knowledge check

**Which statement best describes responsible workplace AI use?**

A. A confident AI response can be used without review.  
B. AI may assist with bounded work, but consequential output should be verified and work outside the user's authority should be escalated.  
C. AI may override a safety or professional requirement if it saves time.  
D. Confidential information may be entered into any AI system if the final output is useful.

**Expected answer:** B.

### Error identification

An AI draft states that a learner is “professionally certified in healthcare AI” because the learner completed a short workforce workshop.

**Expected principle:** course completion must not be presented as professional certification, licensure, accreditation, or scope of practice unless a separate authorized credentialing process establishes that status.

### Source verification

An AI summary states a percentage improvement that is not contained in the supplied source data.

**Expected principle:** remove the claim or calculate/verify it from an authoritative source before use. Polished language is not evidence.

### Privacy decision

A participant is about to paste a document containing personal identifiers and confidential employer information into a public AI service.

**Expected principle:** stop; do not enter restricted information into an unapproved system. Use approved training data or an approved tool under applicable policy.

## Common instructor rubric

Each domain is scored 0–2 by an authorized instructor.

- **2 — Demonstrated:** accurate, specific, supported, appropriately bounded, and independently explainable.
- **1 — Developing:** core principle is present but incomplete or requires correction.
- **0 — Not demonstrated:** materially inaccurate, unsafe, unsupported, fabricated, or treats AI as final authority where human review is required.

| Domain | 2 — Demonstrated | 0 — Not demonstrated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Uses only supported facts and corrects material AI error | Repeats fabricated or unsupported information |
| Verification | Identifies an authoritative source or accountable person and checks consequential output | Accepts consequential AI output without meaningful review |
| Privacy / confidentiality | Removes or withholds restricted/unnecessary information | Treats confidential data as unrestricted AI input |
| Human authority / escalation | Correctly identifies who owns the consequential decision | AI or learner assumes authority not actually granted |
| Documentation | Records the final action and evidence truthfully | Final record is misleading, invented, or incomplete |
| Accountability | Can explain why the final human action is defensible | Treats the AI response itself as proof |

### Proposed demonstration threshold

Illustrative only, pending final institutional approval: **10/12 with no zero in Privacy, Human Authority, or Accountability**.

This threshold is not SCWDB-approved until accepted during implementation/curriculum approval.

## Example Instructor Guide

### Session title

Responsible AI in Healthcare Operations — Review Before Action

### Recommended duration

120 minutes within the broader 6–8 hour Healthcare Industry Accelerator pathway.

### Objectives

Participants should be able to:

- explain a major limitation of generative AI;
- write a useful prompt while minimizing sensitive disclosure;
- identify unsupported or fabricated AI output;
- verify consequential facts before action;
- distinguish administrative assistance from licensed/professional authority;
- escalate work outside their role;
- produce truthful corrected documentation.

### Suggested agenda

- 15 min — AI foundations and limitations
- 20 min — safe prompting, privacy, cybersecurity, and employer policy
- 20 min — fact-checking and error-detection demonstration
- 35 min — Virtual Clinic Lab activity
- 20 min — instructor-led rubric review and debrief
- 10 min — knowledge check and completion instructions

### Facilitation prompts

The instructor should guide reasoning rather than reveal an answer immediately:

- “What source supports that statement?”
- “What would you verify before acting?”
- “Is that decision inside this role?”
- “What information could be removed from the prompt?”
- “Who retains authority for the next action?”
- “How would you document the verification?”

### Instructor authority

The instructor controls pacing, release of exercises, rubric interpretation, feedback, final grading, and completion. AI-generated commentary may support discussion but cannot independently establish participant competence or completion.

### Accessibility and accommodations

The assessed objective is responsible decision-making, not speed, fine-motor control, or a specific input method. Where appropriate, support:

- live captions/transcripts through the approved delivery platform;
- keyboard-only operation and visible focus;
- screen-reader-compatible distributed documents;
- enlarged text, zoom, and reflow;
- additional response time where approved;
- accessible text alternatives to pointer-only/visual-only interactions;
- verbal or typed responses where response mode is not the learning objective.

Accommodation records should contain only the minimum information needed for administration.

### Instructor calibration

Before first delivery, instructors should score at least two sample submissions using the common rubric and resolve material scoring disagreement. Changes to scoring anchors should be versioned and documented.

This is an engineering and delivery commitment, **not a claim of formal WCAG certification or independent conformance audit**.

## Program data and security statement

- Ordinary Klinikos EDU training is synthetic-data-first.
- Real patient PHI is not required for standard healthcare exercises.
- Participant or employer restricted information must not be entered into public/unapproved AI systems.
- Final registration, attendance, survey, retention, transfer, and reporting methods must follow SCWDB-approved systems and contractual requirements.
- Instructor-only answer keys and privileged program data must not be unnecessarily exposed to learner/browser surfaces.
- Existing authentication, authorization, institution scoping, and human-review rules remain authoritative.

---

## Human–AI authority statement

Klinikos EDU may use AI to support drafting, practice, critique, explanation, and scenario interaction. AI does not independently:

- diagnose, prescribe, or authorize care;
- establish professional scope, licensure, privileges, or credential status;
- certify professional competence;
- fabricate participant qualifications, work history, outcomes, or references;
- submit job applications by default;
- grant platform permissions;
- determine final grades or completion where instructor review is required;
- approve safety, legal, financial, contractual, HR, or employer-policy decisions.
