# Klinikos EDU — Representative Workforce Training Materials

Status: **Proposed representative materials for proposal/demo use. Not SCWDB-approved curriculum.**

These materials are intentionally canonical and should be adapted from this source rather than copied into multiple contradictory proposal documents. They are designed to demonstrate the existing Klinikos EDU delivery model: live instructor-led teaching, synthetic applied simulation, human-reviewed assessment, and explicit responsible-AI boundaries.

### Instructor message

AI is a tool for drafting, organizing, reviewing, and practicing. It is not an independent source of truth, a licensed clinician, a credentialing authority, or a substitute for workplace policy and authorized human judgment.

## 2. Sample participant activity

### Activity: Review Before Action

**Role:** Medical Assistant  
**Environment:** Synthetic Virtual Clinic Lab  
**Time:** 25–35 minutes

A synthetic patient record shows an overdue referral, incomplete intake paperwork, and a scheduling conflict. An AI-generated administrative summary is provided. The summary includes one unsupported factual claim and one recommendation that crosses the learner's role boundary.

The learner must:

1. identify the unsupported claim;
2. identify the role-boundary violation;
3. correct the administrative summary using only facts present in the scenario;
4. explain what should be escalated and to whom;
5. document what information should not be pasted into an unapproved external AI tool;
6. submit the corrected work for instructor review.

No real patient information is required or permitted for the normal exercise.

## 3. Sample assessment items

### Knowledge check

**Question:** Which statement best describes responsible use of AI in healthcare operations?

A. AI output can be used without review when the model sounds confident.  
B. AI may assist with administrative work, but important output should be verified and work outside the user's authority must be escalated.  
C. AI can make regulated clinical decisions when a staff member is busy.  
D. Patient information may be placed into any AI tool if it improves productivity.

**Expected answer:** B

### Error identification

An AI draft states that a participant is "HIPAA certified" because they completed a privacy lesson. Identify the problem and rewrite the statement truthfully.

**Expected principle:** Course completion must not be represented as professional certification, licensure, accreditation, or scope of practice.

### AI-output critique

Learners receive an intentionally flawed AI response and identify:

- unsupported claims;
- privacy concerns;
- missing verification;
- decisions that require human authority;
- corrections required before use.

## 4. Sample rubric

| Area | Meets expectation | Needs revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Uses only facts supported by the scenario and corrects the AI error | Repeats unsupported or invented information |
| Verification | Explains how important output was checked | Accepts AI output without meaningful review |
| Privacy | Identifies information that should not be disclosed to an unapproved AI tool | Treats confidential data as unrestricted input |
| Role boundary | Escalates work outside the assigned role | Makes or endorses decisions outside the assigned role |
| Documentation | Records a clear, truthful operational action | Documentation is incomplete, misleading, or fabricated |
| Human oversight | Preserves instructor/supervisor/clinician authority where required | Treats AI as final authority |

Final grading and competency determinations remain instructor-controlled. Model-generated commentary may assist review but cannot independently establish completion or competence.

## 6. Example instructor guide

### Session title

Responsible AI in Healthcare Operations — Review Before Action

### Recommended duration

120 minutes

### Session objectives

By the end of the session, participants should be able to:

- distinguish appropriate operational AI assistance from work requiring human authority;
- apply safe-prompting and minimum-disclosure principles;
- identify hallucinations and unsupported claims;
- correct an AI-generated administrative draft;
- explain when escalation is required;
- document the review process accurately.

### Suggested agenda

- 15 min — introduction and AI foundations
- 20 min — privacy, cybersecurity, and safe prompting
- 20 min — hallucination/error-detection exercise
- 35 min — Virtual Clinic Lab activity
- 20 min — instructor-led debrief and rubric review
- 10 min — knowledge check and completion instructions

### Instructor authority

The instructor controls lesson pacing, scenario release, rubric interpretation, feedback, final grading, and completion. AI-generated suggestions are advisory and reviewable.

## 7. Accessibility statement

Klinikos EDU is being built toward WCAG 2.2 AA-oriented practices where practical, including keyboard operation, visible focus, semantic headings and tables, form labels and errors, zoom/reflow, reduced-motion support, mobile layouts, and non-color-only status communication. This statement is an engineering direction and does **not** claim formal WCAG certification or independent conformance audit.

For live instruction, program operators should select delivery tools and operating practices that support reasonable accessibility needs such as captions/transcripts and accessible distributed documents.

## 8. Program data and security statement

The normal Klinikos EDU experience is synthetic-data-first. Real patient data is not required for standard training or demonstration. Institutional data must remain scoped to the applicable organization/program and role. Important instructor/admin changes should be auditable. Logs, analytics, sample scenarios, and proposal demonstrations must not contain PHI or secrets.

No payment, program enrollment, AI response, or demo state may override existing Klinikos authentication, authorization, tenant isolation, or human-review rules.

## 9. Human–AI authority statement

Klinikos EDU uses AI to support drafting, practice, critique, feedback preparation, and scenario interaction. AI does not independently:

- diagnose or prescribe;
- authorize care;
- establish licensure or scope of practice;
- certify professional competence;
- fabricate participant qualifications;
- submit job applications by default;
- grant platform permissions;
- determine final grades or completion without the required human authority.

Instructor review remains authoritative for grading and competency evidence.
