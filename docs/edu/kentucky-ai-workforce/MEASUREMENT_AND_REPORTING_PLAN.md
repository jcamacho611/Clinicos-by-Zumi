# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Measurement and Reporting Plan

Status: **proposal/capture working document**. This is a Klinikos design for the SCWDB response, not an SCWDB-approved reporting specification. Final measures, fields, cadence, and formats remain subject to the RFP, posted addenda, contract negotiation, and SCWDB approval.

## Purpose

The program should prove more than attendance. It should show whether participants can use AI more responsibly and effectively in realistic workforce tasks while preserving human judgment, privacy, accuracy, and appropriate escalation.

Klinikos will not manufacture outcomes, infer competence from attendance alone, or claim that AI independently determines participant competency. Instructor judgment remains authoritative.

## Measurement principles

1. **Measure observable behavior, not marketing claims.** Evidence should come from participant work, scored assessments, instructor observations, and completion records.
2. **Pre/post comparison where practical.** Use the same or equivalent constructs before and after instruction to measure change without teaching to a single memorized item.
3. **Human-reviewed scoring.** Automated tools may assist future workflows only after governed AI capability is approved; they do not independently release grades or completion evidence.
4. **Synthetic/approved training data only.** Ordinary training exercises do not require real patient data, employer secrets, or other sensitive production records.
5. **Minimum necessary participant data.** Collect only the participant/program data required for eligibility, delivery, reporting, support, and audit obligations established by SCWDB and applicable law.
6. **No unsupported certification claims.** Completion evidence documents participation and demonstrated training outcomes; it is not professional licensure, clinical credentialing, or employer authorization.
7. **Accessibility is part of delivery quality.** Accommodation needs, inaccessible materials, and remediation actions should be tracked as operational quality signals without exposing unnecessary disability information.

## Core outcome domains

The proposed measurement model maps to the responsible-AI and workforce behaviors SCWDB has already described across the two services.

| Domain | Observable participant behavior | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI limitations | Identifies when AI may be incomplete, inaccurate, stale, biased, or unsuitable for a task | Short knowledge check; scenario explanation |
| Task selection | Distinguishes appropriate AI-assisted work from work that requires human authority or a different tool | Task-classification exercise |
| Safe prompting | Gives sufficient context while avoiding unnecessary sensitive/confidential data | Prompt revision activity |
| Privacy/confidentiality | Recognizes data that should not be entered into an unapproved AI tool | Scenario decision + rationale |
| Accuracy/fact-checking | Verifies material AI output against an independent or authoritative source before action | Verification log / source citation |
| Cybersecurity awareness | Recognizes suspicious links, credential requests, unsafe uploads, or policy violations | Scenario response |
| Human oversight | Identifies the responsible human decision-maker and escalation point | Escalation decision |
| Documentation | Records what was checked, changed, escalated, or approved without inventing facts | Participant work product |
| Workplace productivity | Uses AI to improve a bounded administrative/workforce task without bypassing required controls | Role-based practical exercise |
| Career readiness | Produces truthful AI-assisted resume/application/interview or professional-communication work | Before/after artifact |

## Healthcare pathway measurement

Healthcare training remains administrative and operational. It does not train participants to use AI for diagnosis, prescribing, treatment selection, independent clinical judgment, or professional scope decisions.

The healthcare demonstration should measure whether a participant can:

1. recognize that a synthetic workflow problem is appropriate for bounded AI assistance;
2. avoid entering unnecessary or prohibited sensitive information;
3. create or improve a prompt for a scheduling, follow-up, documentation-support, information-organization, or communication task;
4. detect at least one unreliable, incomplete, or unsupported AI output;
5. verify the material fact or instruction independently;
6. identify the point at which human authority is required;
7. escalate appropriately;
8. complete the corrected operational action; and
9. document the evidence supporting the final action.

The existing `Review Before Action` representative exercise should be used as the baseline demonstration pattern.

## Pre/post model

A practical pre/post instrument should cover the same five constructs without relying on identical memorized answers:

1. **Understand:** AI capabilities, limitations, and common failure modes.
2. **Direct:** safe, effective, appropriately scoped prompting.
3. **Protect:** privacy, confidentiality, security, and employer-policy boundaries.
4. **Evaluate:** fact-checking, verification, source quality, and error detection.
5. **Own:** human review, escalation, accountability, and documentation.

Recommended proposal metric examples, subject to SCWDB approval:

- change in average pre/post knowledge score;
- percentage of participants correctly identifying a privacy-sensitive prompt;
- percentage correctly identifying an AI error requiring verification;
- percentage correctly choosing a human-escalation path;
- practical-exercise rubric distribution by domain;
- completion rate among participants who begin instruction;
- participant confidence/self-efficacy change, reported separately from demonstrated skill;
- participant satisfaction and instructor-quality feedback.

Do not commit to a numerical improvement target until baseline, cohort mix, service duration, and SCWDB performance expectations are known.

## Practical exercise rubric

Use the proposal's 0–2 scoring model consistently:

- **0 — Not demonstrated:** unsafe, unsupported, omitted, or materially incorrect.
- **1 — Developing:** partially correct but incomplete, weakly evidenced, or requiring instructor correction.
- **2 — Demonstrated:** correct, adequately evidenced, appropriately bounded, and independently explainable by the participant.

Recommended critical domains:

- Privacy / Minimum Disclosure
- Verification / Fact-Checking
- Human Authority / Escalation
- Truthful Documentation

A participant should not be treated as successfully completing a practical scenario while scoring `0` in a critical domain, even if the aggregate score would otherwise pass. Final completion rules remain subject to SCWDB approval.

## Delivery metrics

Track operational delivery separately from learning outcomes.

| Metric | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Sessions scheduled / delivered | Prove delivery against authorized schedule |
| Live remote vs in-person sessions | Support delivery-mode reporting |
| Participant starts | Denominator for completion analysis |
| Participant completions | Program delivery/completion measure |
| Attendance duration or attendance status | Support participation evidence where required |
| Cancellations / reschedules | Identify delivery friction and capacity effects |
| Instructor assigned | Accountability and staffing traceability |
| Accommodation/material issue logged | Accessibility QA without unnecessary sensitive detail |
| Material/version used | Ensure participants were evaluated against the intended curriculum version |
| Assessment version | Preserve scoring consistency and auditability |

Do not treat SCWDB's approximately 980-participant planning figure as guaranteed volume or a required denominator.

## Continuous-improvement cycle

Use a bounded human-owned improvement loop:

`deliver → collect evidence → review outcomes/issues → identify curriculum or facilitation change → approve change → version materials → deliver next cohort`

Changes that may affect learning objectives, assessment difficulty, completion requirements, accessibility, safety boundaries, or reporting definitions should be versioned and reviewed before use.

Recommended review cadence:

- **After each cohort/session:** delivery issues, instructor notes, accessibility issues, recurring learner confusion.
- **Monthly during active delivery:** outcome trends, completion, cancellations, instructor calibration, material defects.
- **Quarterly or contract-defined:** consolidated performance review, approved curriculum changes, risk register, staffing/capacity review.

Do not silently change a scenario, rubric, or assessment after learners have been evaluated against it.

## Instructor QA and calibration

To reduce grading drift across instructors:

- provide common facilitator notes and scoring anchors;
- review sample responses at onboarding;
- calibrate critical-domain scoring before independent delivery;
- require a brief rationale for overrides or unusual scores where practical;
- monitor material scoring inconsistencies across cohorts;
- update examples/versioned guidance rather than relying on undocumented verbal rules.

No AI system should independently resolve instructor-scoring disputes or certify participant competency.

## Reporting package

The proposal should offer a practical reporting package without claiming integrations or automated exports that have not been verified.

### Proposed recurring report

Subject to SCWDB approval, include:

- reporting period;
- authorized sessions delivered;
- participant starts and completions;
- delivery mode and service/pathway;
- pre/post aggregate results where available;
- practical-assessment aggregate results by domain;
- participant feedback summary;
- instructor/quality issues and corrective actions;
- accessibility issues and remediation status;
- curriculum/material versions used;
- risks, blockers, and upcoming delivery plan.

### Participant-level evidence

Where contractually required and legally permitted, maintain bounded evidence such as:

- participant/program identifier;
- service/pathway;
- attendance/completion state;
- assessment result;
- practical-exercise result;
- completion evidence date/version.

Final participant identifiers, retention rules, transmission method, and record layout must be agreed with SCWDB. Do not promise LTI, SCORM, xAPI, SSO, or direct workforce-system integration unless separately implemented and verified.

## Reporting implementation truth

### Supportable now / by existing architecture

- instructor-owned grading and completion boundaries;
- synthetic role-based practical exercises;
- scenario/material versioning principles;
- assessment/evidence model;
- institution/course/cohort isolation in the EDU specification;
- participant completion/certificate concepts with non-licensure boundary;
- manual or prepared aggregate reporting from approved program records when automation is not yet implemented.

### Configuration/build work expected before first participant delivery

- SCWDB-specific data dictionary;
- final pre/post instrument;
- final completion rules;
- SCWDB-approved report template and cadence;
- institutional/cohort configuration;
- approved participant identifiers and retention/transmission rules;
- pathway-specific assessment mapping outside the existing healthcare foundation;
- reporting UX/exports required by the final contract.

### Must not be claimed as currently live without separate evidence

- automatic SCWDB workforce-system integration;
- real-time SCWDB dashboards;
- LTI 1.3 / SSO / SCORM / xAPI production integration;
- automated AI grading or competency determination;
- AI-generated scenarios/feedback while the governed Klinikos AI Gateway remains unavailable;
- formal WCAG 2.2 AA conformance certification;
- historical participant outcome data.

## Data and security posture

For ordinary instruction and demonstration:

- use synthetic or approved non-sensitive training data;
- prohibit real patient data in healthcare exercises;
- prohibit employer secrets, credentials, confidential documents, or other unnecessary sensitive data in prompts;
- keep instructor-only answer keys/rubrics outside learner-facing payloads;
- validate inputs server-side where the application handles program data;
- apply institution/course/cohort access boundaries;
- preserve human attribution for consequential grade/completion changes;
- define final retention, export, and deletion obligations from the awarded contract rather than guessing them in advance.

Security-training content and engineering controls are not themselves a security certification.

## Proposal win theme

**Klinikos EDU measures whether a participant can perform responsibly, not whether they sat through a presentation.**

The program combines live instruction with synthetic applied practice, evidence-based assessment, human-authoritative scoring, and a bounded continuous-improvement loop. That supports SCWDB's workforce-readiness goals while preserving truthful product, privacy, accessibility, and human-oversight boundaries.
