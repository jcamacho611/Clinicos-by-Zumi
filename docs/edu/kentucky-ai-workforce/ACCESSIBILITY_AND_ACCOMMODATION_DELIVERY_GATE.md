# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Accessibility & Accommodation Delivery Gate

Status: **capture and delivery control**. This document is not a legal opinion, accessibility certification, or claim of WCAG conformance. It defines the evidence and operating controls that must exist before Klinikos represents that the proposed workforce program can serve participants with disabilities equitably in live-remote, in-person, platform-supported, assessment, and reporting workflows.

## Why this is a bid gate

SCWDB operates a federally funded workforce system, publicly maintains an Accessibility and Reasonable Accommodations Policy, and states that it conducts annual physical and program accessibility assessments of local one-stop centers. The Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network also requires live remote and in-person delivery, participant evidence, assessment, reporting, and optional use of a proprietary technology platform.

For this bid, accessibility cannot be reduced to “the website supports keyboard navigation.” The service itself must be deliverable in an accessible manner.

## Governing posture

Klinikos will design the program to support equal access, effective communication, reasonable modifications, and appropriate auxiliary aids/services where required by the executed contract and applicable law.

The proposal must **not** claim:

- formal WCAG 2.2 AA conformance unless a qualified audit proves it;
- that every accommodation can be fulfilled without advance notice;
- that the platform itself determines whether a person is legally entitled to an accommodation;
- that a participant must disclose a diagnosis to an instructor to receive an approved accommodation;
- that an accommodation may lower a safety, privacy, verification, or human-authority requirement when that requirement is the construct being assessed.

## 1. Accommodation operating model

The final delivery workflow should be:

`SCWDB / participant accommodation signal → minimum-necessary accommodation record → assigned accommodation owner → instructor/facilitator briefing on need-to-know basis → accessible delivery → evidence that accommodation was provided → issue/escalation path → retention/disposition under approved policy`

### Minimum-necessary principle

Ordinary instructors should receive the accommodation instructions necessary to deliver the class, not medical histories or unnecessary disability details.

Examples:

- “Provide live captions and written instructions” is appropriate operational information.
- A diagnosis, medical record, or unrelated disability history is not ordinary instructor-facing information.

The exact intake, approval, documentation, and retention workflow remains subject to SCWDB policy and the executed contract.

## 2. Live-remote delivery requirements

Before proposal signature, Klinikos must define how live-remote delivery supports, where applicable:

- keyboard-only participation;
- screen-reader-compatible instructional materials;
- captioning for instructor speech and required video/audio content;
- accessible chat/Q&A participation;
- accessible links and downloadable materials;
- visible focus and understandable form errors;
- non-color-only status/feedback;
- sufficient contrast and zoom/reflow behavior;
- reduced-motion preferences;
- alternate participation method when a specific interaction method is not itself the learning objective;
- additional response/process time when approved without changing the skill actually being assessed;
- accessible breakout-room instructions and facilitator support;
- a non-video participation alternative when camera use is not an essential instructional requirement.

If a third-party live-meeting platform is used, its accessibility characteristics and limitations must be documented. Selection of a meeting platform cannot silently expand Klinikos claims about the accessibility of Klinikos itself.

## 3. In-person delivery requirements

Before Klinikos promises statewide in-person delivery, the operating plan must address:

- accessible training location or an approved equivalent arrangement;
- accessible entrances, routes, seating, and restroom access as applicable to the site;
- instructor awareness of the accommodation plan without unnecessary medical disclosure;
- accessible print/digital alternatives;
- communication aids/services arranged through the responsible party when required;
- assistive listening / captioning / interpreter coordination where applicable;
- seating and room configuration that does not isolate a participant;
- additional time or modified delivery method when approved and educationally appropriate;
- technology/power/connectivity needs for assistive devices where known;
- a same-day escalation contact for an accommodation failure.

Klinikos must not promise that every third-party venue is accessible until the specific site is verified or SCWDB confirms the site/arrangement.

## 4. Accessible instructional materials

Proposal and delivery materials should use accessible source formats wherever practical rather than relying on screenshots or flattened image-only documents.

Required practices include:

- meaningful heading hierarchy;
- descriptive link text;
- readable tables with proper headers;
- text alternatives for instructional images where the visual is not itself the assessed construct;
- captions/transcripts for prerecorded instructional audio/video;
- sufficient contrast;
- no information conveyed by color alone;
- plain-language instructions where complexity is not the learning objective;
- accessible document exports where those exports are required participant resources.

Any proposal deck or participant workbook must be checked independently from the web application; a visually strong deck is not automatically an accessible deck.

## 5. Assessment accommodation rule

The governing principle is:

> Preserve the assessed construct; adapt the access method.

Examples:

### Usually supportable when approved

- extended time;
- screen-reader-compatible question delivery;
- keyboard entry instead of mouse interaction;
- typed response instead of handwriting;
- oral response or scribe support when writing mechanics are not the assessed skill;
- captions/transcripts;
- scheduled breaks;
- accessible alternate file format.

### Must not silently change the competency being assessed

If the practical assessment is specifically evaluating whether a participant can:

- recognize unsafe disclosure;
- verify an AI output;
- distinguish human from AI authority;
- preserve an employer/safety boundary;
- escalate an unsafe or uncertain recommendation;

then the accommodation may change how the participant perceives or communicates the answer, but it should not supply the substantive answer or remove the required safety judgment.

Instructor scoring remains human-authoritative. AI may not decide that an accommodation was appropriate or determine competency from disability status.

## 6. Instructor and facilitator readiness

Before a named instructor can be presented as statewide-ready, the delivery plan must establish that the instructor receives practical guidance on:

- following approved accommodation instructions;
- using accessible presentation practices;
- reading aloud relevant visual information when appropriate;
- monitoring captions/communication channels rather than assuming they work;
- providing materials in the approved accessible format;
- escalating failures immediately;
- avoiding unnecessary requests for medical/disability information;
- protecting accommodation information from disclosure to other participants;
- preserving the assessed construct while adapting delivery.

Backup instructors must be capable of following the same accommodation plan.

## 7. Platform disclosure boundary

Klinikos EDU currently targets practical WCAG-oriented behaviors including semantic structure, keyboard operation, visible focus, programmatic labels, live status announcements, contrast, target sizing, mobile/reflow behavior, and non-color-only communication.

The proposal must distinguish:

1. **implemented behavior that has been tested**;
2. **behavior defined by the governing product specification**;
3. **buyer-specific delivery accommodation**;
4. **formal conformance/certification not yet established**.

No proposal language may collapse those four categories into “WCAG certified” or an equivalent unsupported claim.

## 8. Accommodation evidence and privacy

The institutional data model should capture only the minimum operational evidence required by SCWDB/contract policy, such as:

- accommodation requested/communicated flag where appropriate;
- operational accommodation category or instruction;
- responsible owner;
- fulfillment status;
- issue/escalation status;
- date/time evidence;
- delivery/session reference.

It should not become a shadow medical-record system.

Participant accommodation information must not be exposed in employer feedback, ordinary analytics, public reports, peer views, or unrelated instructor surfaces.

Reporting to SCWDB should use aggregate accessibility/service indicators unless participant-level detail is specifically authorized and required.

## 9. Delivery failure / escalation

An accommodation failure is a service-delivery issue, not a reason to mark the learner absent, incomplete, or unsuccessful by default.

The operational sequence should be:

`issue detected → preserve participant access where possible → notify responsible coordinator → provide approved accessible alternative / reschedule if necessary → document service impact → protect participant from adverse treatment caused by contractor failure → review root cause → update delivery controls`

No automated system may deny completion or produce an adverse participant outcome merely because an accessibility feature failed.

## 10. Pre-submission evidence checklist

Before proposal signature, capture must be able to answer yes to all applicable items:

- [ ] SCWDB accommodation/request-routing responsibility is identified or explicitly held for contract clarification.
- [ ] The proposal names an accommodation/accessibility owner for Klinikos delivery.
- [ ] Live-remote captioning/communication approach is specified.
- [ ] Required participant files can be provided in accessible source/export formats.
- [ ] Instructor guide contains accessibility/accommodation delivery instructions.
- [ ] Assessment alternatives preserve the assessed construct.
- [ ] In-person venue/accessibility responsibility is allocated between SCWDB, host site, and contractor.
- [ ] Same-day accommodation issue escalation is defined.
- [ ] Accommodation information is minimum-necessary and access-controlled.
- [ ] Participant reporting does not expose accommodation/disability information without authorization.
- [ ] Third-party platform accessibility dependencies are disclosed.
- [ ] No unsupported WCAG/ADA/accessibility certification claim appears in proposal or demo.
- [ ] Evaluator demo can be operated by keyboard and at mobile/zoom/reflow conditions used in verification.
- [ ] Representative slides/workbooks/certificate examples receive their own accessibility review.

Any unresolved item must be labeled as a contract-dependent assumption rather than silently represented as complete.

## 11. Evaluator-facing win theme

The proposal should not merely say “accessibility is important.” It should show that accessibility is part of the operating model:

> Klinikos separates the learning objective from the access method. Participants are expected to meet the same responsible-AI, verification, privacy, safety, and human-oversight standards, while the program can adapt how instruction and evidence are delivered so disability-related access needs do not become unrelated barriers to learning.

This is particularly important in a dislocated-worker program where participants may enter with different technology familiarity, communication needs, disabilities, and prior educational experiences.

## Source basis reviewed for this gate

Primary public sources reviewed during capture include:

- SCWDB Policies, Plans & Procedures page, which publishes an Accessibility and Reasonable Accommodations Policy.
- SCWDB “What is a Workforce Development Board?” page, which states that SCWDB conducts annual physical and program accessibility assessments of local one-stop centers.
- U.S. Department of Justice ADA Title III regulations §36.303, including course modifications and auxiliary aids/services.
- U.S. Department of Justice web accessibility guidance covering keyboard access, accessible forms, captions, effective communication, and accessible electronic information.
- U.S. Department of Justice Title II web/mobile accessibility rule guidance. Technical applicability and contract responsibility must be confirmed for the final SCWDB delivery arrangement; this document does not independently determine legal coverage.

## Final rule

**Accessibility is a delivery capability and evidence obligation, not a marketing adjective.**

If Klinikos cannot prove an accessible delivery path for a required participant interaction, the proposal must disclose the limitation, provide a credible accessible alternative, or treat it as a pre-launch requirement. It must never convert that limitation into an unsupported conformance claim.
