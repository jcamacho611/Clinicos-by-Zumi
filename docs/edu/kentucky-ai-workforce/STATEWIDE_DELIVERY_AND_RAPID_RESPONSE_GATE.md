# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Statewide Delivery & Rapid Response Gate

Status: **proposal qualification and operating-capacity gate**. This document does not claim current Kentucky staffing, statewide deployment history, an award, or SCWDB approval.

## Why this is a bid gate

SCWDB's posted Q&A states that nine of Kentucky's ten Local Workforce Development Areas, representing 97 counties, have provided Letters of Support. Live remote instruction is expected to be the routine delivery mode, while in-person delivery may be requested strategically for Rapid Response events, significant layoffs, plant closures, or other concentrated participant needs.

An out-of-state prime may serve, but it must demonstrate sufficient instructor availability, local/regional deployment capacity, travel responsiveness, continuity, administrative capacity, and financial capacity.

A proposal that describes only software or curriculum is therefore incomplete.

## Required proof before proposal signature

The proposal may describe statewide/multi-region capability only after all of the following are documented with real evidence:

- named lead instructor(s) for Career Readiness;
- named lead or qualified instructional coverage for all five Industry Accelerator pathways;
- named backup coverage for each service family;
- current city/state or practical travel origin for each in-person-capable instructor;
- documented availability assumptions through the initial contract period;
- which instructors are employees, contractors, or proposed subcontractors;
- role, qualification, and responsibility of every proposed subcontractor;
- realistic remote-concurrency capacity;
- realistic in-person concurrency capacity;
- instructor reassignment and absence process;
- travel-booking/payment responsibility;
- cash-flow ability to front approved travel/instructor/subcontractor costs until reimbursement;
- minimum lead time the proposed team can responsibly commit to for a requested in-person event;
- process for handling two or more simultaneous or overlapping Rapid Response requests;
- accessible-delivery coverage when accommodations affect materials, technology, or staffing;
- escalation contact for an event that cannot be staffed within the proposed service standard.

Do not invent names, Kentucky presence, contractor commitments, travel times, availability, or financial capacity to make this section appear complete.

## Proposed operating model

### Routine delivery

Use live remote instruction as the default scalable operating mode, consistent with SCWDB's current planning assumptions. The model should support frequent scheduled cohorts without requiring participants to purchase software, equipment, AI subscriptions, or special technology unless expressly approved and funded.

### Strategic in-person delivery

Use in-person delivery for approved Rapid Response events, layoffs, plant closures, concentrated cohorts, accessibility needs, or other circumstances identified by SCWDB/LWAs.

The final proposal should state a **proposed response standard only after the named staffing/travel evidence supports it**. Do not promise a statewide arrival SLA based on software alone.

### Demand spikes

When two or more regions request overlapping delivery, the operating order is:

1. confirm authorized request, location, cohort size, pathway, accommodations, and required date;
2. check qualified primary and backup instructor capacity;
3. activate an approved subcontractor/pathway specialist only if already disclosed and permitted;
4. propose live-remote delivery when appropriate and accepted by SCWDB;
5. offer the earliest compliant alternate date when qualified coverage is unavailable;
6. escalate any service-risk condition to the contract/program lead rather than assigning an unqualified instructor.

No demand spike may lower instructor qualification, accessibility, privacy, safety, or evidence requirements.

## Rapid Response event checklist

Before accepting an in-person delivery request, confirm:

- authorized SCWDB/LWA request;
- requested pathway/service and learning objectives;
- participant estimate and approved class-size approach;
- site address and room/device/connectivity assumptions;
- accessibility/accommodation requirements;
- instructor and backup confirmation;
- travel approval/cost basis under the contract;
- participant registration/data-transfer method;
- approved curriculum/material version;
- attendance/completion evidence method;
- incident/escalation contacts;
- contingency if weather, instructor illness, site failure, or connectivity prevents delivery.

## Financial-capacity link

Statewide capacity is not credible if Klinikos cannot finance it between delivery and reimbursement.

Before final pricing/signature, the working-capital model must test at least:

- one in-person event plus normal remote delivery;
- two concurrent in-person events in different regions;
- one Rapid Response event requiring travel on short notice;
- 30-, 45-, 60-, and 90-day collection timing;
- instructor/subcontractor payment obligations due before SCWDB payment;
- unreimbursed or capped travel exposure.

Expected contract revenue, the 980-participant planning target, speculative investment, or uncommitted credit may not be treated as available working capital.

## Truthful evaluator language

Preferred framing before all staffing evidence is complete:

> Klinikos proposes a remote-first statewide delivery model with strategically deployable in-person capacity for Rapid Response and concentrated workforce events. Final service-response commitments will be supported by named instructional staffing, backup coverage, approved subcontractor disclosures where applicable, travel assumptions, and demonstrated administrative/financial capacity in the proposal.

Do not state that Klinikos already maintains a Kentucky instructor network, statewide field team, or 97-county deployment footprint unless verified evidence exists.

## Final gate

Before proposal signature, a skeptical evaluator must be able to answer **yes** to all:

1. Are real qualified instructors named for both services and all five pathways?
2. Is backup coverage real and documented?
3. Can the team support routine remote delivery at the proposed cadence?
4. Can the team credibly reach in-person events without invented geography or staffing?
5. Is overlapping-region demand addressed without lowering quality?
6. Are subcontractors fully disclosed and their roles/qualifications evidenced?
7. Are travel assumptions reflected in Appendix B and working-capital modeling?
8. Can Klinikos finance instructors, subcontractors, and travel while awaiting payment?
9. Are accessibility and participant-data requirements preserved in every delivery mode?
10. Is every service-level promise supported by current staffing evidence rather than aspiration?

If any answer is no, the proposal must narrow the promise, add verified capacity, or alter the bidder/team structure before submission.
