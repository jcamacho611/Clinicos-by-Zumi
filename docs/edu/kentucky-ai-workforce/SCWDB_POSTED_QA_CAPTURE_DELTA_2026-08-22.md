# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Posted Q&A Capture Delta

Status: **capture/source-of-truth working document** for the September 18, 2026 proposal. Rechecked on 2026-08-26 against the currently posted SCWDB Questions & Answers dated 2026-08-25. This file is not a substitute for the RFP and must be reconciled against the September 4 addenda before submission.

## Governing capture rule

When an older internal assumption conflicts with the posted Q&A or a later addendum, the buyer's current published requirement wins.

## 1. Full-scope proposal is required

SCWDB expects a coordinated proposal covering both:
- Service A — AI Industry Accelerator; and
- Service B — AI-Powered Career Readiness.

Service A must address all five occupational pathways:
1. Manufacturing
2. Construction
3. Logistics
4. Healthcare
5. Business Operations

**Klinikos consequence:** healthcare may remain the strongest existing simulation foundation, but the proposal must provide a credible outline and sample lesson segment for every pathway.

## 2. Participant volume and pathway mix are not guaranteed

The approximately 980 unique participants are a planning target, not a guaranteed minimum. SCWDB has not established a fixed distribution between Service A and Service B or across the five pathways.

**Klinikos consequence:** staffing, pricing, cash flow, and platform capacity must survive uneven and materially lower demand. Never represent 980 participants as guaranteed revenue.

## 3. Remote-first with credible in-person capacity

Nine of Kentucky's ten Local Workforce Development Areas, representing 97 counties, have provided Letters of Support, subject to award/funding/implementation. SCWDB expects live-remote instruction to be the routine model and in-person delivery to be used strategically for Rapid Response events, major layoffs, plant closures, and similar needs.

**Klinikos consequence:** sell a remote-first operating model with documented regional/in-person deployment capacity, not a travel-heavy default.

## 4. Proprietary platform use is allowed, with disclosures

A proprietary career-readiness technology platform may be used during instruction and may remain available afterward. Required participant access must be included in proposed pricing unless otherwise identified/approved. Participants cannot be required to buy software, subscriptions, equipment, or technology as a condition of participation unless expressly approved and funded.

The proposal should disclose the platform's:
- instructional purpose;
- licensing requirements;
- access duration;
- accessibility features;
- data-retention practices;
- privacy protections;
- associated costs.

**Klinikos consequence:** EDU can be a differentiator, but proposal language must separate built capability from pre-launch configuration and cannot imply unsupported integrations, automated teaching, or automated certification.

## 5. Protect pre-existing Klinikos IP

SCWDB does not intend to require transfer of ownership of pre-existing proprietary software, curriculum, instructional methodologies, platforms, tools, or independently developed IP. The proposal should distinguish:
- pre-existing Klinikos IP;
- third-party licensed products;
- Kentucky-specific newly developed/materially customized deliverables.

**Klinikos consequence:** maintain the IP schedule and ensure the final contract does not accidentally convert pre-existing Klinikos software/methodology into transferred work product.

## 6. Appendix B is completion-based

The posted Q&A expressly refers to **per-completed-participant pricing**. SCWDB expects a uniform completion standard to be established during implementation/curriculum approval, and bidders should recommend criteria/documentation including:
- verified attendance;
- participation in required activities;
- assessments or demonstrations of learning;
- other appropriate successful-completion criteria.

Registration or partial attendance alone is not sufficient for payment. The contractor must maintain completion records sufficient for participant-level invoicing, grant monitoring, and audit.

**Klinikos consequence:** customer-facing pricing must follow Appendix B. Internal fixed/cohort/platform/travel economics must be converted into commercially survivable per-completion pricing rather than submitted as a nonresponsive startup-style model.

## 7. Out-of-state prime and teaming are permitted

A U.S. out-of-state for-profit corporation may serve as prime if it can meet the RFP's live-remote, in-person, instructor, administrative/financial, reporting, accessibility, data-security, and compliance requirements. Subcontractors/teaming partners are permitted, subject to disclosure/approval, while the prime remains responsible for performance, records, reporting, QA, compliance, payment, and continuity.

**Klinikos consequence:** Kentucky physical-office presence is not the gate. Delivery capacity, qualification evidence, finances, and prime accountability are.

## 8. All five pathways need proposal-time evidence

The proposal must address all five pathways and include an outline and sample lesson segment for each. Final approved curricula for both workshops/all pathways must exist before participant delivery. Specialist staffing may scale based on actual demand.

**Klinikos consequence:** do not confuse proposal samples with a claim that every specialized production curriculum/simulator is already complete.

## 9. Class-size and pricing assumptions must be explicit

SCWDB has not set one mandatory cohort size or minimum in-person frequency. Respondents should identify recommended, minimum, and maximum class sizes for live-remote and in-person delivery and clearly identify assumptions affecting price, including:
- cohort size;
- travel;
- minimum enrollment;
- cancellation charges;
- other material conditions.

**Klinikos consequence:** these assumptions belong in Appendix B/pricing narrative and the internal stress model.

## 10. Performance extends beyond attendance

SCWDB emphasizes outcomes within the contractor's reasonable control, including:
- participant satisfaction and skill gain;
- instructional quality/relevance;
- instructor effectiveness;
- scheduling reliability;
- accessibility and engagement;
- timely/accurate reporting;
- continuous improvement from participant/employer feedback;
- privacy, data, documentation, and grant compliance.

The broader Network employment/paid-work goal depends on multiple parties.

**Klinikos consequence:** never guarantee job placement as a contractor-controlled outcome. Measure and improve what Klinikos and instructors can actually influence.

## 11. Organizational experience can be satisfied through documented team continuity

SCWDB now expressly states that a recently formed entity may rely on clearly documented relevant experience of key personnel, affiliated entities, predecessor organizations, and substantive teaming/subcontracting partners. Appendix C references may include comparable engagements performed by those parties when the proposal clearly identifies who actually performed the work, that party's relationship to Klinikos, the role of the proposed key personnel, and the scope of the comparable services.

An experienced organization does not have to be the prime solely because it contributes qualifying instructional or technical experience. However, the prime must still prove current administrative, financial, management, staffing, systems, and contract-management capacity.

**Klinikos consequence:** the three-year requirement is no longer a categorical new-company exclusion. Build the qualification narrative around truthful continuity and actual performing parties, while never attributing another person's or organization's prior work to Klinikos itself.

## 12. Healthcare is operational/responsible-AI, not regulated clinical decision support

SCWDB describes the healthcare pathway around responsible AI literacy and practical administrative/operational use, including communication, scheduling, documentation support, information organization, privacy, verification, cybersecurity/confidentiality, employer policy, human review, and appropriate limits. No particular EHR or licensed clinical-decision workflow is required.

**Klinikos consequence:** the synthetic Virtual Clinic Lab is a relevant differentiator when demonstrated as operational training. Do not turn the bid demo into unsupported clinical decision automation.

## 13. A fully mature LMS is not required at proposal submission

Representative proposal samples include slides, participant activities, assessment items, certificate/badge examples, and an instructor guide. Final curricula/materials/accessibility review/completion requirements can be finalized during implementation before first delivery.

**Klinikos consequence:** demonstrate credible product evidence now and a disciplined 30-day implementation path; do not claim unbuilt institutional integrations or formal accessibility certification.

## 14. Common AI foundation plus pathway-specific modules is expressly acceptable

SCWDB accepts a common foundational AI curriculum shared across pathways for topics such as responsible AI, prompting, verification, privacy, cybersecurity, bias awareness, human oversight, and appropriate workplace use, provided each pathway has meaningful occupation-specific applications, exercises, scenarios, and examples.

**Klinikos consequence:** keep one reusable instructional core and layer healthcare, manufacturing, construction, logistics, and business-operations applied modules on top. Do not build five unrelated curriculum systems.

## 15. Employer validation may occur during implementation

SCWDB does not require completed employer validation for every pathway at proposal submission. It is acceptable to propose a documented employer/industry validation process to be completed with SCWDB-designated employers or industry representatives during implementation.

**Klinikos consequence:** describe a rigorous validation workflow now; do not fabricate employer endorsements or letters that do not exist.

## 16. Contractor-issued competency credentials are acceptable

SCWDB does not require a third-party or externally accredited credential. A contractor-issued competency-based digital badge or microcredential may satisfy the strongly preferred credential enhancement if the proposal clearly describes:
- competencies represented;
- earning criteria;
- completion/competency verification;
- employer-shareable or verifiable record behavior; and
- associated platform/licensing/participant cost.

A certificate of completion remains mandatory.

**Klinikos consequence:** Klinikos can use its own governed completion evidence and certificate/badge architecture without buying a third-party credential solely to satisfy responsiveness. Do not describe the badge as licensure or external accreditation.

## 17. Do not assume every participant has a device, email account, or AI subscription

SCWDB warns bidders not to assume every participant will have an individual computer, active email account, personal AI subscription, or ability to create a new technology account. In-person hosts will generally coordinate a location with standard internet/basic presentation equipment when available, but technology will vary.

The proposal must identify required devices, connectivity, accounts, software/platforms, licenses/subscriptions, and specialized equipment, and must identify corresponding costs.

**Klinikos consequence:** make the training resilient to shared-device or limited-account conditions. Required Klinikos/AI access should be included or explicitly priced, and participants must not be forced to buy subscriptions or equipment.

## 18. Key personnel must be named; every future instructor does not

SCWDB expects the proposal to identify key personnel responsible for contract management, curriculum development, instructional leadership, QA, and other major functions. It does not require every instructor/SME who may serve during the contract to be named at proposal submission, and qualified replacements/additions may be used during implementation subject to contract requirements and approval.

**Klinikos consequence:** lock the core named team and document the recruitment/replacement/coverage model instead of treating an exhaustive multi-year instructor roster as a submission blocker.

## 19. Separate implementation, completion, in-person, travel, technology, and optional pricing is allowed

SCWDB permits separate pricing for:
- one-time curriculum development/customization/implementation/launch;
- per-completed-participant delivery;
- alternative cohort/session pricing;
- in-person delivery;
- approved travel;
- technology/software/licensing/digital credentials; and
- other clearly separated optional services.

Final payment terms are negotiable and subject to federal funding requirements.

SCWDB further confirms that occasional in-person instructor travel/lodging does **not** have to be buried inside the per-completed-participant rate; approved travel may be separately identified.

**Klinikos consequence:** the current Appendix B structure is directionally aligned. Preserve separate implementation, unit, in-person, and approved-travel economics and make every assumption explicit.

## 20. Cancellation/minimum-enrollment terms are ours to propose

SCWDB has not established a mandatory minimum participant threshold or cancellation window. Respondents should state their own minimum/preferred/maximum class sizes, minimum enrollment if any, minimum session charges, cancellation/rescheduling windows, charges if any, and low-enrollment pricing conditions.

**Klinikos consequence:** finish a flexible but margin-safe low-volume policy before final Appendix B. Do not assume minimum cohort revenue will be guaranteed.

6. Is product maturity separated from pre-launch configuration?
7. Are pre-existing IP and Kentucky-specific deliverables separated?

9. Is the three-year qualification supported by real attributed experience or an acceptable documented teaming structure?
10. Are key personnel, subcontractors, backup coverage, and replacement processes clearly distinguished?
