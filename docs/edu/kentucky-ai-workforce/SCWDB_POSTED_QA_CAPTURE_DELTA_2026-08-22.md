# SCWDB Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network — Posted Q&A Capture Delta

Status: **capture/source-of-truth working document** for the September 18, 2026 proposal. This file records proposal implications from SCWDB's currently posted Kentucky AI Workforce Readiness Network Questions & Answers. It is not a substitute for the RFP or future September 4 addenda. Reconcile this file against every later posted addendum before submission.

## Why this delta matters

The posted Q&A resolves several assumptions that materially affect responsiveness, pricing, platform disclosures, curriculum evidence, staffing, IP treatment, reporting, and cash-flow risk. These answers must override any older internal capture assumption that conflicts with them.

## 1. Full-scope response is required

SCWDB expects one coordinated proposal covering both:

- Service A — AI Industry Accelerator; and
- Service B — AI-Powered Career Readiness.

The proposal must address all five Service A occupational pathways:

1. Manufacturing
2. Construction
3. Logistics
4. Healthcare
5. Business Operations

**Capture consequence:** Klinikos may lead with healthcare as its strongest existing simulation foundation, but the proposal cannot present the other four pathways as optional future add-ons. Each pathway needs a credible initial outline and sample lesson segment at proposal time, with final approved curricula completed before participant delivery.

## 2. Demand is variable across services and pathways

SCWDB has not established a distribution between Service A and Service B or among the five occupational pathways. The approximately 980 unique participants remain a planning target, not a guaranteed minimum. Some participants may take one service; others may take both.

**Capture consequence:** capacity, staffing, curriculum configuration, and pricing must remain viable under uneven demand. Do not model 980 participants as guaranteed revenue or assume an even five-pathway split.

## 3. Delivery geography is broad, but live remote is expected to dominate

Nine of Kentucky's ten Local Workforce Development Areas, representing 97 counties, have provided Letters of Support. Final participation remains subject to grant award, funding, and implementation agreements.

SCWDB expects live-remote instruction to be the most common routine delivery model. In-person delivery is expected strategically for Rapid Response events, large layoffs, plant closures, and similar concentrated needs.

**Capture consequence:** the proposal should present a remote-first operating model with credible rapid in-person deployment capacity rather than a travel-heavy default model.

## 4. Proprietary platform use is expressly permitted

SCWDB permits a proprietary career-readiness technology platform during instruction and may permit continued participant access after completion, provided required access is included in the proposed price and participants are not required to purchase software or subscriptions.

The proposal should disclose:

- instructional purpose;
- licensing requirements;
- duration of participant access;
- accessibility features;
- data-retention practices;
- privacy protections; and
- associated costs.

Any platform must comply with applicable SCWDB privacy, data-security, accessibility, and participant-information requirements.

**Capture consequence:** Klinikos EDU may be positioned as the practice/delivery layer supporting live instructors, but the proposal must truthfully state what platform functions are live versus configuration/build work before first delivery. Required participant access must be economically included in the proposed pricing unless SCWDB approves otherwise.

## 5. Pre-existing Klinikos IP does not have to be transferred

SCWDB states that it does not intend to require transfer of ownership of pre-existing proprietary software, curriculum, instructional methodologies, platforms, tools, or other independently developed IP.

The proposal should clearly distinguish:

### A. Pre-existing Klinikos IP

Examples may include, where verified:

- Klinikos EDU software foundation;
- Virtual Clinic Lab architecture;
- reusable workforce-AI literacy primitives;
- pre-existing curriculum methods and instructional frameworks;
- pre-existing platform code, data models, security controls, and design systems.

### B. Third-party licensed products or platforms

Identify only actual third-party dependencies used in delivery and state their applicable restrictions/cost treatment.

### C. Kentucky-specific new or materially customized deliverables

Examples may include:

- SCWDB-specific pathway lesson segments;
- SCWDB-specific instructor guides;
- SCWDB-specific participant materials;
- approved reporting layouts/data dictionaries;
- Kentucky-specific implementation/configuration artifacts.

Ownership, licensing, reuse, modification, statewide access, and post-contract use of Kentucky-specific deliverables will be addressed during contract negotiation.

**Capture consequence:** include an explicit IP schedule in the proposal and contract-review checklist so pre-existing Klinikos IP is not accidentally treated as work-made-for-hire or transferred by implication.

## 6. Appendix B requires per-completed-participant pricing

SCWDB's Q&A expressly refers to **per-completed-participant pricing requested in Appendix B**.

SCWDB expects a uniform completion standard to be finalized during implementation/curriculum approval. Bidders should recommend completion criteria and documentation practices, including:

- attendance requirements;
- participation in required activities;
- assessments or demonstrations of learning; and
- other appropriate successful-completion criteria.

SCWDB states that participant-level payment will depend on verified completion records sufficient for invoicing, grant monitoring, and audit. Registration or partial attendance alone is not a completed participant for payment purposes.

**Mandatory pricing implication:** even if the internal cost architecture uses fixed implementation, cohort/session, instructor-day, platform, travel, and participant cost drivers, the submitted Appendix B must provide the requested **per-completed-participant rate(s)** in the format required by the RFP/addenda.

Do not let the internal blended cost model become a nonresponsive customer-facing pricing structure.

### Required internal conversion model

For each proposed per-completed-participant rate, model the underlying economics across at least:

- low utilization;
- expected utilization;
- high utilization;
- Service A only completion;
- Service B only completion;
- participants taking both services where applicable;
- live-remote versus in-person delivery mix;
- minimum/recommended/maximum class sizes;
- cancellation/reschedule exposure;
- instructor bench and QA/admin load;
- travel exposure; and
- 30/45/60/90-day collection timing.

If SCWDB permits separate non-participant implementation or travel line items in a later addendum/contract, use them only as expressly allowed. Otherwise ensure the per-completed-participant price recovers the full expected cost and risk without relying on guaranteed volume.

## 7. Completion must be measurable and auditable

Klinikos should recommend a completion standard built around observable participation and learning evidence, while labeling it proposed until SCWDB approves the final standard.

The existing capture approach should continue to require:

- verified attendance;
- required activity completion;
- assessment/practical evidence;
- human instructor scoring/approval where applicable;
- completion evidence version/date; and
- sufficient records for invoicing and audit.

Do not define completion as attendance alone.

## 8. All five pathways need proposal-time evidence

SCWDB requires respondents to address all five pathways and provide an outline and sample lesson segment for each pathway. Final approved curricula must exist before participant delivery.

A scalable staffing model may activate specialists based on demand, but the proposal still must show a credible and sufficiently developed delivery approach for every pathway.

**Capture consequence:** manufacturing, construction, logistics, and business-operations content may remain truthfully labeled configurable methodology in the product, but the proposal must include buyer-specific pathway outlines and sample lesson segments before submission. Those proposal samples are not the same claim as saying the full production curricula already exist.

## 9. Class-size and scheduling assumptions must be explicit

SCWDB has not established a standard cohort size or minimum in-person frequency. Respondents should identify recommended, minimum, and maximum class sizes for live-remote and in-person instruction.

Pricing assumptions should clearly identify:

- cohort size;
- travel;
- minimum enrollment;
- cancellation charges; and
- other conditions affecting price.

**Capture consequence:** final pricing must state these assumptions clearly, even if the customer-facing Appendix B is per completed participant.

## 10. Performance emphasis is broader than attendance

SCWDB expects particular emphasis on:

- participant satisfaction;
- demonstrated or perceived skill gain;
- instruction quality/relevance;
- instructor effectiveness;
- scheduling reliability;
- accessibility;
- participant engagement;
- timely and accurate reporting;
- continuous curriculum improvement using participant/employer feedback; and
- compliance with data, privacy, documentation, and grant requirements.

The broader Network goal includes employment or paid work-based learning within six months of enrollment, but SCWDB recognizes that employment outcomes depend on multiple parties beyond the contractor.

**Capture consequence:** do not promise job placement as a contractor-controlled outcome. Measure and report what Klinikos/instructors can directly influence while supporting the broader employment goal.

## 11. Organizational experience remains a qualification gate

SCWDB's currently posted Q&A states that the **organization must have at least three years of relevant experience** in adult education, workforce training, career-readiness instruction, AI/digital-skills training, or closely related services.

Individual instructors do not have a prescribed degree/license requirement, but proposed instructional staff must demonstrate relevant AI literacy, workforce, industry, facilitation, and adult-learning capability.

Subcontractors/teaming partners are permitted and may be useful for pathway specialization or distributed in-person delivery, but the prime remains responsible for performance, compliance, records, QA, reporting, and subcontractor payment.

**Capture consequence:** do not weaken or reinterpret the three-year requirement without the pending formal SCWDB clarification. Build the evidence matrix from real key-personnel/team history, and keep a qualified teaming path ready if the final interpretation requires organizational history the prime cannot independently demonstrate.

## 12. Healthcare pathway fit is now formally supported

SCWDB confirms the healthcare pathway is primarily responsible-AI literacy and practical administrative/operational workplace use, not regulated clinical decision-making.

Illustrative areas include:

- nonclinical/administrative workflows;
- communication;
- scheduling;
- documentation support;
- information organization;
- privacy awareness;
- human review;
- fact-checking and verification;
- cybersecurity/confidentiality;
- employer policy; and
- appropriate limits on AI use.

SCWDB does not require a particular EHR, clinical AI system, or licensed clinical-decision workflow.

**Capture consequence:** the current synthetic healthcare simulation foundation is a genuine differentiator. Keep the demonstration operational and nonclinical.

## 13. Proposal-time technology maturity requirement is achievable

An existing LMS or fully developed proprietary content platform is not required at proposal submission.

Required representative samples include:

- slides;
- participant activities;
- assessment items;
- certificate or badge examples; and
- an example instructor guide.

The proposal also must explain technical approach, curriculum design, tools, delivery, engagement, staffing, and implementation. Final curriculum/materials/accessibility review/completion requirements may be finalized and approved during implementation before first participant delivery.

**Capture consequence:** sell what exists plus a credible 30-day implementation/configuration plan. Do not claim unbuilt institutional integrations, automated teaching, automated grading, or completed non-healthcare curricula.

## Skeptical-evaluator test

Before submission, an evaluator should be able to answer **yes** to each of these from the proposal without inference:

1. Does this bidder cover both required services and all five pathways?
2. Can it deliver live remotely statewide/multi-region and deploy in person when needed?
3. Does Appendix B provide the requested per-completed-participant pricing?
4. Is completion objectively documented rather than equated with registration/attendance?
5. Are class-size, cancellation, travel, and minimum-enrollment assumptions explicit?
6. Is the bidder clear about what technology exists now versus what will be configured before launch?
7. Are proprietary IP and Kentucky-specific deliverables clearly separated?
8. Is the healthcare pathway operational/nonclinical and aligned to SCWDB's stated intent?
9. Does the bidder have a credible three-year qualification story or approved teaming solution?
10. Are accessibility, privacy, data security, reporting, QA, and continuous improvement treated as delivery requirements rather than marketing claims?
