# Pricing research snapshot — 2026-08-12

Purpose: preserve the external-market reasoning behind Klinikos pricing V1 without making competitors the product source of truth.

## Observed models
- Jane publishes low monthly practice-management pricing ($54 / $79 / $99 base tiers) and separately prices add-ons such as AI Scribe, telehealth, insurance billing, and websites. Lesson: modular add-ons and per-practitioner expansion are normal, but Klinikos should not anchor its implementation-heavy operating-system offer to Jane's commodity SaaS price point.
- DrChrono uses quote-based Foundation / Growth / Premium packaging, with metered/per-unit elements and paid add-ons. Lesson: higher-complexity EHR/RCM software can preserve pricing flexibility and meter external-cost services.
- NexHealth packages Scheduling, Forms, Communications, and Payments and asks practices to configure a package/quote; it supports monthly or annual billing and contract-buyout selling. Lesson: sell consolidation and workflow outcomes, not a giant feature list.
- athenahealth explicitly says its pricing corresponds to customer collections. Lesson: outcome-aligned economics are credible in healthcare software, but any success/revenue-share arrangement needs legal/commercial review.
- Zocdoc currently charges no upfront Marketplace subscription and charges a one-time fee when a new patient books; published examples indicate booking fees can vary materially by specialty/location. Lesson: marketplace liquidity can be strengthened by low upfront friction and value-event monetization.

## Klinikos conclusion
Use a hybrid model:
1. Paid analysis and implementation to monetize expertise and cover high-touch deployment.
2. Premium recurring clinic subscription tied to operational scope rather than a cheap self-service seat.
3. Customer-funded allowances/prepaid/overage for variable external vendor costs.
4. Add-ons for materially distinct outcomes/integrations.
5. Grid basic entry at $0 to grow supply, optional Pro subscriptions, and resource-class-specific completed-transaction economics around a 10% testing midpoint where lawful and commercially appropriate.
6. Enterprise custom pricing for complexity, governance, integration, and scale.

## Guardrail
This research is directional competitive evidence, not legal advice and not permission to use percentage-based healthcare fees in a structure that would violate fee-splitting, anti-kickback, corporate-practice, payer, tax, or marketplace rules. Resource-class pricing must remain configurable and reviewed before regulated launch.
