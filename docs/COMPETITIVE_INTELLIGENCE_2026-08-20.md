# Klinikos Competitive Intelligence — 2026-08-20

Status: current external market evidence for commercial convergence

Purpose: give product, pricing, sales, and Claude Code a dated primary-source view of the competitive frame Klinikos is entering. This is not a feature-parity checklist and does not authorize copying competitor claims or pricing.

## Executive finding

The phrase **clinic/practice operating system** is strategically useful but is not, by itself, a defensible category claim.

Tebra now publicly describes its platform as an operating system for practice success. athenahealth markets an AI-native all-in-one platform and explicitly argues that consolidation lowers total cost. Practice Fusion, CharmHealth, and eClinicalWorks create much lower monthly EHR price anchors than Klinikos Core.

Therefore Klinikos cannot win simply by saying:

- AI-native;
- all-in-one;
- operating system;
- lower software fragmentation.

Those positions are already occupied in whole or in part.

Klinikos must make the differentiated job unmistakable:

> **Klinikos is the clinic operating system that owns the work between systems — unfinished follow-up, handoffs, referrals, readiness, revenue work, and external capacity — with Zumi helping teams understand and coordinate the next action.**

The strongest long-term differentiation is the combination of:

1. operational continuity and unfinished-work ownership;
2. human-governed Zumi intelligence embedded in those workflows;
3. Grid as an external healthcare capacity/network layer;
4. measurable customer-stack economics;
5. accumulated workflow and operating intelligence that improves coordination without turning clinical authority over to AI.

## Primary-source market anchors

All links below were reviewed on 2026-08-20.

### Practice Fusion

Official pricing:
https://www.practicefusion.com/pricing/

Public anchor:
- starts at $199/month per provider;
- required annual commitment;
- includes three signing staff licenses and unlimited non-signing staff;
- integrated billing services are marketed separately/additionally;
- public onboarding messaging says a practice can be fully up and running in two weeks or less.

Strategic implication for Klinikos:
A clinic buyer can easily compare Klinikos Core at $995/month with a $199/provider EHR unless Klinikos explains the much larger operating scope and displaced stack. Public copy must prevent a false EHR-vs-EHR comparison.

### CharmHealth

Official pricing:
https://www.charmhealth.com/ehr/ehr-pricing-us.html

Public anchors:
- free limited plan;
- $0.50 per encounter with a $25 monthly minimum;
- $200/provider/month fixed provider plan.

Strategic implication for Klinikos:
Low-cost and usage-based entry points already exist. Price leadership is not a defensible Klinikos strategy. Klinikos must justify price through broader operating value and evidence.

### eClinicalWorks

Official pricing:
https://www.eclinicalworks.com/products-services/pricing/

Public anchors:
- EHR Only: $449/month per provider;
- EHR with Practice Management: $599/month per provider;
- RCM as a Service: 2.9% of practice collections;
- public page advertises no startup costs and free migration from another EHR.

Strategic implication for Klinikos:
A multi-provider clinic can already buy a credible EHR + PM bundle at a price much closer to Klinikos Core. Implementation cost becomes a direct objection because eClinicalWorks explicitly promotes no startup costs and free migration. Klinikos must explain what the implementation fee buys and why implementation creates operating value rather than merely switching on software.

### Tebra

Official pricing overview:
https://www.tebra.com/pricing/overview

Official pricing policy:
https://www.tebra.com/tebra-pricing-policy

Public anchors reviewed:
- overall range approximately $49–$799 per provider/month depending on bundle and practice type;
- physician Practice Essentials: $599/provider/month;
- physician Practice Automation: $799/provider/month;
- non-physician standard bundles: $399–$449/provider/month;
- EHR Starter and Billing Starter each start at $199/provider/month for eligible low-volume physicians;
- AI Note Generation: $99/month/provider up to 400 notes, with overage pricing disclosed in Tebra's pricing policy;
- onboarding, migration, transactional, messaging-compliance, and other fees may exist under the published policy.

Category signal:
Tebra publicly describes its products as forming the **complete operating system for practice success** and markets bundled EHR, billing, telehealth, patient experience, automation, and practice-growth capabilities.

Strategic implication for Klinikos:
The generic phrase “operating system” cannot be treated as the moat. Klinikos needs a more precise ownable promise. The most credible wedge is not “all clinic software in one place”; it is **closing the operational loops that remain unresolved across the clinic and its external network**.

### athenahealth / athenaOne

Official athenaOne page:
https://www.athenahealth.com/solutions/athenaone

Official cost/value page:
https://www.athenahealth.com/why-choose-us/cost-value

Public positioning:
- AI-powered / AI-native all-in-one EHR, medical billing/practice management, and patient engagement;
- pricing tied to a percentage of collections;
- explicitly markets lower total cost by reducing the cost and frustration of multiple solutions, relationships, integrations, and technology;
- markets minimal upfront costs and no long-term contracts;
- promotes a large healthcare integration/network footprint.

Strategic implication for Klinikos:
“AI + all-in-one + lower total cost” is already a major incumbent message. Klinikos must not rely on those phrases as differentiation. The defensible story has to center on workflow ownership, next-action accountability, cross-system continuity, Grid network effects, and evidence-driven economics.

## Competitive frame Klinikos should use

Do not frame the sale as:

> Klinikos vs. EHR.

Frame it as:

> **Klinikos vs. the fragmented operating model around the EHR.**

For many clinics, an EHR may remain a required connected system during transition. Klinikos should prove value in the work that gets lost between systems and people while progressively replacing point solutions where product truth and customer economics justify it.

## Competitive kill test

Before a public message or pricing recommendation is approved, answer:

1. Why would a clinic buy Klinikos instead of Tebra Practice Automation?
2. Why would a clinic buy Klinikos instead of eClinicalWorks EHR + PM?
3. Why would a clinic pay implementation fees when competitors advertise low/no startup cost?
4. Why not use the existing EHR plus ChatGPT or another ambient/AI assistant?
5. Which software subscriptions can Klinikos actually replace today?
6. Which systems must remain connected today?
7. What unfinished work does Klinikos make visible that competitors do not clearly own?
8. What does Grid make possible that a conventional EHR/PM bundle does not?
9. What evidence proves the customer saves money, time, or operational leakage?
10. What increases switching cost or retention without trapping the customer or compromising data portability?

If these answers are weak, improve the product or message rather than hiding the weakness behind design language.

## Homepage implication

The first fold should not stop at “AI-native clinic operating system.” That phrase explains the category but does not establish a moat.

The first fold should immediately communicate a distinctive outcome such as:

> **Run the clinic. Close the loops.**
>
> Klinikos gives every follow-up, referral, handoff, readiness gap, revenue task, and capacity need a visible next action — with Zumi helping your team coordinate the work and Grid connecting the outside capacity the clinic still needs.

This is a direction, not mandatory final copy. Preserve plain language and truth.

## Pricing implication

Current Klinikos anchors should not be changed from competitor prices alone.

Internal pricing must model:

- safe cost-to-serve floor;
- customer current stack;
- replaceable stack cost;
- non-replaceable connected cost;
- implementation burden;
- customer economic value;
- Klinikos contribution margin;
- buyer payback;
- competitive alternatives.

For example, a three-provider physician clinic could see public alternatives ranging from roughly $600/month for three Practice Fusion licenses to approximately $1,797/month for three eClinicalWorks EHR+PM licenses or $1,797–$2,397/month for three Tebra physician bundle licenses before add-ons/other fees. These are directional arithmetic comparisons from public list prices, not total-cost claims. Klinikos must use the buyer's actual configuration and actual vendor quotes before presenting savings.

## What Klinikos should not copy

Do not race to reproduce every competitor feature.

Do not chase parity on:

- every specialty template;
- every billing service;
- every marketing feature;
- every integration;
- every AI note feature;
- every EHR certification surface at once.

Instead prioritize capabilities that strengthen the wedge:

- open-loop detection;
- ownership and escalation;
- cross-workflow next actions;
- human-governed Zumi assistance;
- referral/follow-up closure;
- revenue-work readiness;
- clinic-stack analysis;
- external capacity discovery through Grid;
- measurable operating visibility;
- migration/connection pathways that reduce switching fear.
