## 3. Application-only authority

If a lender requires a Board resolution merely to submit an application before final terms are known, use a narrower resolution:

> RESOLVED, that `[AUTHORIZED OFFICER]` is authorized to submit an application to `[LENDER]` for financing not to exceed `$[AMOUNT]`, to provide truthful company financial/corporate information, and to receive/negoti­ate a financing proposal; provided that this application authority **does not authorize acceptance, borrowing, a personal guaranty, a lien, a security interest, a confession of judgment, or other binding credit obligation without further Board approval where required by this resolution or law.**

This is the preferred structure when the objective is to preserve negotiating control.

## 4. Personal guaranty boundary

A Board resolution cannot sign an individual's personal guaranty for them.

If a lender requires a personal guaranty:

- identify the actual required guarantor(s) based on executed ownership/control;
- review scope, continuing-guaranty language, waiver provisions, collateral, spousal issues, and release conditions;
- obtain the guarantor's separate informed signature;
- do not represent a proposed shareholder as a guarantor merely because a lender template assumes 20% ownership.

## 5. UCC / collateral boundary

Before granting a security interest:

- identify exact collateral;
- search/reconcile existing liens where appropriate;
- understand blanket-lien language;
- preserve ability to finance future receivables/IP/equipment where strategically important;
- determine whether IP is included directly or through after-acquired/general-intangible language;
- review cross-defaults and affiliate guarantees.

Material liens and guarantees justify counsel review.

## 6. SBA / CDFI truth law

SBA participation or guaranty does not mean:

- Klinikos is approved;
- repayment is optional;
- owner guarantees are waived;
- collateral rules disappear;
- a government agency is lending the full amount directly.

The lender/program's actual note, guaranty, and closing documents control.
