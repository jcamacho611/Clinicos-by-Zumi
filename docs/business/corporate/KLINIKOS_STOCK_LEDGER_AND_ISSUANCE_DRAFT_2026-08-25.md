# 5. NON-CASH / IP CONSIDERATION REVIEW

BCL §504 permits several forms of consideration, including money, tangible/intangible property, services actually received, and certain binding obligations. That statutory permission does **not** make a founder/IP transaction tax-neutral or strategically correct.

Before using founder-created Klinikos IP or affiliate-owned assets as share consideration, reconcile:

- who currently owns the asset;
- provenance and creation date;
- whether contractors/AI/vendor licenses affect rights;
- fair/agreed value and Board record;
- tax consequences;
- Section 351 eligibility if relevant;
- founder basis/holding-period implications;
- QSBS implications where relevant;
- related-party accounting;
- existing liens/licenses;
- whether assignment or license is superior;
- whether 888 OPS OPES COPIA is involved.

**Attorney + CPA/tax review is justified before executing a material IP-for-stock transaction.**

# 6. RESTRICTED FOUNDER STOCK / VESTING REVIEW

If any founder stock is subject to reverse vesting or repurchase rights, do not use a generic stock issuance resolution alone.

Prepare a separate restricted stock purchase agreement addressing:

- vesting start;
- vesting schedule;
- credit for prior service if approved;
- cliff if any;
- company repurchase option;
- termination treatment;
- death/disability;
- transfer restrictions;
- ROFR/co-sale/drag rights only if deliberately adopted;
- IP/confidentiality obligations;
- tax reporting;
- **83(b) election timing where applicable**.

The federal 83(b) deadline is consequential and should be confirmed from current IRS rules at execution; do not rely on a stale template.
