## Scope response structure

### 1. Recruitment and outreach

Proposed approach:
- coordinate with MMVWB/City referral channels and Lawrence community partners;
- use multilingual/community-aware outreach where approved;
- maintain referral-source and outreach-channel evidence;
- avoid claiming partner commitments until written permission exists.

### 2. Assessment and enrollment

Use structured intake to capture eligibility evidence supplied/approved by MMVWB, baseline digital/AI familiarity, career goals, accessibility needs, and readiness for training. Do not independently invent eligibility rules outside the RFP/contract.

### 3. Individual service strategy / case management

Use a participant plan that records next action, owner, target dates, training milestones, work-based-learning status, credential status, job-search activity, placement status, and follow-up obligations. Human workforce staff remain authoritative for eligibility and consequential participant determinations.

### 4. AI training model

Proposed common foundation plus applied modules:
- AI fundamentals and responsible use;
- prompt design and task decomposition;
- generative AI for workplace productivity;
- data literacy and analytics fundamentals;
- AI-supported research and verification;
- privacy, security, hallucination/error detection, and employer-policy boundaries;
- introductory automation/workflow concepts;
- applied cybersecurity and safe data handling;
- role-appropriate exposure to machine-learning concepts without overstating software-engineering proficiency.

Hands-on learning should use synthetic/non-sensitive scenarios and artifact-producing exercises, with instructor review rather than model authority over competency.

### 5. Career readiness

Reuse the governed Klinikos career-readiness module:
- responsible AI-assisted resume support without fabrication;
- applications and professional communication;
- interview preparation;
- prompt basics;
- fact checking;
- privacy and employer policy;
- honest explanation of AI-assisted work;
- recognizing inaccurate or unsafe model output.

### 6. Work-based learning

Design a five-tier progression compatible with the RFP, from career exposure and employer interaction through job shadowing/project work, internship/work experience, and employment/apprenticeship pathways. Do not claim employer slots until written commitments exist.

### 7. Credential / certificate

Klinikos can provide evidence-backed program completion and competency certificates/badges with explicit non-licensure language. The final proposal must identify any third-party industry credential only when cost, eligibility, exam requirements, and delivery responsibility are verified.

### 8. Placement and 12-month follow-up

Track employment/postsecondary status, retention checkpoints, participant contact attempts, employer feedback where authorized, and required long-term outcomes without presenting unavailable earnings data as known.

## Participant incentive and stipend economics

The RFP requires the provider budget to account for participant milestone incentives totaling up to $350 per participant:
- enrollment/orientation: $75;
- AI training milestone: $75;
- credential milestone: $75;
- work-based-learning milestone: $75;
- final employment/career-readiness milestone: $50.

The RFP also requires $15/hour stipends for in-person classroom participation/program activities. MMVWB/City funds the stipends, but bidder budget treatment must reflect the RFP instructions.

These participant payments are pass-through/program costs and must not be confused with Klinikos revenue or margin.
