## 2. Problem answer — Northwestern version

Healthcare teams already have systems of record, but clinicians and nurses still spend significant effort reconstructing workflow state across notes, intake, messages, orders, results, staff handoffs and follow-up queues.

The failure is not only administrative. It directly changes how care teams spend their attention.

A physician should not have to search multiple places to answer:

- What changed since the last visit?
- What did the MA/LPN/RN already capture?
- What result is new or corrected?
- What remains unresolved?
- What needs my authority now?
- What happens after I sign?

Klinikos is focused on reducing that reconstruction burden while preserving human clinical authority.

## 3. Product answer — Northwestern version

The clearest Northwestern wedge is **Current Visit + Clinical Change + Staff Handoff + governed Zumi assistance**.

### Current Visit

One continuous clinical workstage:

`PATIENT SNAPSHOT → WHAT CHANGED → STAFF HANDOFF → TODAY → CLINICAL → ASSESSMENT & PLAN → ORDERS & RESULTS → DOCUMENTATION & CODING → CLOSE VISIT`

### Clinical Change

Klinikos increasingly treats longitudinal medicine as structured comparison rather than a stack of isolated notes:

`INITIAL → PREVIOUS → TODAY`

The system should help the clinician see meaningful changes in symptoms, body-map evidence, results, treatment progress and unresolved work without inventing clinical findings.

### Staff Handoff

MA/LPN/RN work is attributed and handed forward deliberately rather than hidden inside one generic “clinical staff” role.

### Zumi

Zumi can summarize, compare, prepare and identify gaps, but does not become the authority for diagnosis, signature, prescriptions, credential truth or other regulated state.

This is the product story to demonstrate to Northwestern clinicians and operators.

## 6. Competitive advantage — Northwestern version

Most AI products compete at the note or message level.

Klinikos is building around the **full state transition around the visit**:

`before the visit → staff preparation → clinician decision → orders/results → documentation/coding → follow-up/revenue completion`

That creates a different data asset:

- what changed;
- who did what;
- what evidence supported the action;
- what remained incomplete;
- what the clinician accepted/rejected;
- whether the downstream action actually completed.

The company wants to own that governed workflow/evidence layer while remaining interoperable with systems of record and specialized external rails.

## 7. Why now — Northwestern version

The opportunity is not simply “healthcare is adopting AI.”

The deeper timing is:

1. clinical teams are under sustained administrative and operating pressure;
2. AI can now reduce reconstruction/summarization burden;
3. healthcare enterprises are moving from experimentation toward workflow-level AI use;
4. buyers are simultaneously becoming more sophisticated about provenance, authority and risk;
5. Klinikos can build an AI-native operating layer without inheriting decades of UI/workflow assumptions.

That makes **governed workflow intelligence** a stronger position than an unbounded healthcare chatbot.

## 9. Demo emphasis

For Northwestern, the demo should spend less time on Living Home and more time on the clinical sequence.

Recommended 2-minute structure:

**0–15 sec**
Outpatient clinician opens a synthetic patient visit.

**15–45 sec**
Show patient snapshot and `WHAT CHANGED` longitudinal evidence.

**45–70 sec**
Show attributed Staff Handoff from MA/LPN/RN.

**70–95 sec**
Show a new/corrected result and the clinician's actual review responsibility.

**95–110 sec**
Show Zumi preparing a summary or highlighting a documentation gap while clearly remaining assistive.

**110–120 sec**
Show Close Visit and the downstream next-action/revenue truth.

Close:

> “The clinician experiences one visit. Klinikos coordinates the evidence and unfinished work underneath it.”
