## Q4. What problem is your company looking to solve?

### Recommended answer

Outpatient healthcare teams still coordinate consequential work across EHRs, practice-management systems, payer portals, email, phone, fax, spreadsheets and human memory. The problem is not simply missing data. It is that **unfinished work becomes invisible between systems and roles**.

A patient can be scheduled but not ready. A result can arrive but remain unreviewed. A referral can be ordered but never close. A service can be performed but stall before coding, claim acceptance or payment. A clinician can spend a visit reconstructing what changed instead of acting on it. Staff often know pieces of the truth, but no operating layer consistently answers:

**What changed? What matters now? Who owns the next action? Did the action actually complete?**

Klinikos is focused on turning that fragmentation into governed, visible workflow.

### Evidence to preserve

- direct healthcare operations exposure;
- clinician/operator feedback on Current Visit, staff handoff, longitudinal comparison, labs/imaging, coding and revenue workflow;
- current repository workflow/evidence architecture;
- current no-PHI commercial wedge.

---

## Q5. What is your company going to make to solve this problem?

### Recommended answer

Klinikos is building a governed healthcare operating layer that sits across clinical and operational workflows.

Its near-term product centers on three ideas:

1. **A task-first operating environment** that surfaces the work that actually needs attention rather than another generic dashboard.
2. **A converged clinical visit experience** that shows patient context, what changed, staff handoff, orders/results, documentation/coding and visit closure without forcing clinicians through disconnected modules.
3. **Zumi, the intelligence layer**, which can summarize, compare, prepare and coordinate work while deterministic systems retain authority over permissions, clinical signature, credential truth, financial state and other consequential actions.

Klinikos is designed to integrate necessary external rails rather than recreate them. The company wants to own the workflow, state, evidence and next action while labs, payers, payment networks and other specialized systems remain replaceable/connected adapters where appropriate.

Longer term, the same shared primitives support Grid healthcare capacity/resource matching and EDU workforce development, but the first commercial proof is clinic workflow and measurable operational value.

## Q14. Who are your competitors or could become your competitors?

### Recommended answer

Klinikos competes with three categories more than one company:

1. **EHR / practice-management incumbents** such as athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, Tebra and larger enterprise platforms. They own important systems of record and workflow, but clinics still operate across multiple disconnected systems and manual processes.
2. **AI point solutions** such as scribes and workflow assistants that improve one part of clinician work but generally do not own end-to-end operational state, evidence and completion.
3. **The status quo**: staff memory, phone, fax, email, spreadsheets, payer/vendor portals and manual coordination.

Other marketplaces, staffing products, LMS platforms and revenue tools become competitors to later Klinikos expansion layers, but we would not present all of them as direct competitors to the initial clinic workflow wedge.

Klinikos' goal is not to win a checklist contest with Epic. It is to become materially better at coordinating the consequential work that remains fragmented around systems of record.

## Q15. What is your competitive advantage?

### Recommended answer

Our advantage is the operating substrate around the AI, not access to a language model.

Klinikos is building shared healthcare identity, authorization, clinical context, evidence, workflow state, financial truth, audit, configuration and integration boundaries. Zumi operates across the portion of that context the user is authorized to access, while deterministic systems keep authority over signature, credentials, payments, permissions and other consequential state.

That creates several advantages:

- **operator-builder loop:** direct healthcare workflow observation can become product change quickly;
- **workflow completion graph:** Klinikos can understand not only that an event happened, but what should happen next and whether it closed;
- **Clinical Change:** longitudinal evidence can be presented as `initial → previous → today` rather than forcing the clinician to reconstruct change manually;
- **shared primitives:** Clinic OS, Grid, EDU, revenue and future network behavior can compound on the same identity/evidence/relationship architecture instead of becoming disconnected products;
- **capital efficiency:** the company can integrate external rails and activate variable costs after customer value exists rather than recreating every infrastructure network.

Models can change. The governed workflow, evidence, relationships and implementation knowledge remain.

## Q16. Why is now the right time for your startup?

### Recommended answer

Healthcare is simultaneously experiencing greater administrative pressure and faster adoption of AI inside real workflows. The important shift is not simply that generative AI exists. It is that small teams can now build sophisticated workflow intelligence, natural-language interfaces and automation with dramatically less capital, while healthcare buyers are becoming much more demanding about safety, ROI and integration into existing work.

Independent practices are also under operating pressure. AMA data show only 42.2% of physicians were in private practice in 2024, down from 60.1% in 2012, with physicians citing financial strain and administrative burden among the forces affecting practice sustainability.

That creates a narrow opportunity: help outpatient organizations recover operational capacity and make unfinished work visible **without requiring an immediate EHR replacement**, then expand once value is proven.

AI makes the experience possible now; governance, workflow evidence and healthcare operating access make it defensible.
