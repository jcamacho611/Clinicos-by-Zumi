# KLINIKOS EXTERNAL APPLICATION DISCLOSURE REGISTER

Status: GOVERNING CONTROL
Date: 2026-08-27
Applies to: Klinikos, Inc. and 888 OPS OPES COPIA funding, grant, prize, accelerator, procurement, cloud-credit, tax/incentive, economic-development, investor-adjacent, and institutional application workflows.

## 1. Governing law

Every non-public Klinikos fact, file, design, technical detail, roadmap, architecture, internal prompt, algorithm, source/repository artifact, Zumi logic, Grid logic, security detail, pricing/margin model, proprietary research, customer/prospect information, PHI/PII, and internal strategy is CONFIDENTIAL by default.

Minimum necessary disclosure is mandatory.

A grant, prize, accelerator, procurement, or support application is NOT presumed confidential merely because it is an application.

Public information intentionally published on klinikos.io may be reused, subject to accuracy and current-truth verification.

Never disclose real patient data or PHI. Synthetic or redacted data only where needed and lawful.

## 2. Disclosure classes

### PUBLIC-SAFE
May be submitted without special founder approval when accurate and relevant:
- legal company name and verified formation facts;
- public website and intentionally public product positioning;
- high-level problem statement;
- high-level solution description that does not expose reproducible mechanisms;
- target market/customer class;
- public-safe business model/pricing only when already approved for external use;
- high-level milestones and use of funds;
- verified team facts and public biographies;
- public-safe screenshots already intentionally published;
- synthetic, non-PHI examples that do not reveal proprietary internals.

### CONFIDENTIAL - FOUNDER APPROVAL REQUIRED
Do not send, paste, upload, or attach without explicit founder approval and appropriate controls:
- architecture diagrams or system internals;
- proprietary workflow logic, algorithms, prompts, rankings, policy engines, Zumi orchestration, Grid matching/eligibility logic;
- unpublished product roadmap or implementation sequencing;
- security architecture, threat model, controls, credentials, environment detail, or deployment internals beyond minimum procurement/security questionnaires;
- source code or repository artifacts;
- unpublished technical whitepapers, labs, research disclosures, prototypes, design-system internals, or patentable mechanisms;
- proprietary pricing economics, margins, vendor cost model, internal capital strategy, or financial forecasts not specifically approved;
- customer/prospect identities, pipeline, confidential correspondence, or customer-specific workflows;
- any non-public data room material;
- any technical material that would materially help a reviewer reproduce Klinikos.

### PROHIBITED
Never disclose through an ordinary application:
- PHI or real patient records;
- credentials, secrets, tokens, private keys, passwords;
- unnecessary PII;
- proprietary source code unless separately authorized under appropriate legal/IP protections;
- false or unsupported claims.

## 3. Mandatory terms review before submission

Before any application or attachment is submitted, inspect the current submission terms for:
- confidentiality or explicit absence of confidentiality;
- publication/public-posting rights;
- broad licenses to submitted materials;
- rights to modify, create derivative works, sublicense, commercialize, or distribute submissions;
- rights to use submissions/data for AI/model training;
- source-code, data, model-weight, prompt, architecture, or technical-document access requirements;
- IP assignment, option, license, background-IP, foreground-IP, or invention clauses;
- publicity/name/logo/likeness rights;
- warranties, indemnities, releases, waivers, or governing-law commitments.

STOP and obtain founder approval before submission if any term creates a material IP/confidentiality/publication/training/access risk.

Sensitive decks or data rooms require explicit founder approval and, where appropriate, an executed NDA, restricted access, watermarking, expiry, and recipient-specific disclosure.

## 4. Minimum-necessary application rule

Default external application structure:
1. problem;
2. high-level solution;
3. public-safe differentiation;
4. market and customer class;
5. milestones;
6. use of funds/budget;
7. commercialization approach;
8. verified team facts;
9. evidence posture without revealing internals.

Technical narratives must describe WHAT is being achieved and WHY it is difficult without unnecessarily teaching HOW proprietary Klinikos mechanisms work.

For SBIR/STTR and other R&D programs, provide the minimum technical specificity required for scientific/technical evaluation while withholding unnecessary implementation detail, trade secrets, source code, model internals, proprietary prompts, or replicable architecture. If the program requires more, escalate before disclosure.

## 6. Required execution checklist for every external submission

- [ ] Confirm opportunity is still open and viable.
- [ ] Confirm application completion takes priority over new discovery.
- [ ] Verify all factual claims from current evidence.
- [ ] Classify every attachment/answer as PUBLIC-SAFE, CONFIDENTIAL, or PROHIBITED.
- [ ] Strip unnecessary implementation detail.
- [ ] Remove PHI/PII and use synthetic/redacted data only where lawful and needed.
- [ ] Inspect current submission terms and IP/confidentiality/publicity/training clauses.
- [ ] Confirm no broad source/data/model access requirement is being silently accepted.
- [ ] Record exactly what was sent, to whom, why, and under what terms/NDA status.
- [ ] Stop for founder approval on confidential disclosure, binding IP terms, signature, fee, equity, hard credit pull, personal certification, or other reserved authority.
- [ ] Preserve evidence of successful submission/send before changing status to SUBMITTED or SENT.

## 7. Fail-closed rules

If confidentiality status is unclear: classify as CONFIDENTIAL.

If program terms are unavailable or ambiguous on IP/publication/model-training rights: do not send confidential material.

If a reviewer asks for a 'technical deck,' 'architecture,' 'whitepaper,' 'lab,' 'prototype details,' 'data/model access,' 'repository access,' or equivalent: provide only a public-safe high-level response unless founder approval and appropriate legal controls exist.

If a prior artifact was labeled 'non-confidential,' that label alone does not make it safe. Re-review its actual contents against this register before reuse.

## 8. Operating objective

Advance applications aggressively without converting Klinikos trade secrets into free diligence material.

The company should win because reviewers understand the problem, value, market, evidence, milestones, and execution credibility - not because they were given enough proprietary detail to reconstruct the product.
