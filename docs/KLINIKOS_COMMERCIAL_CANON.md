# Klinikos Commercial Canon

Status: active commercial architecture
Last reviewed: 2026-08-20

This document defines the public category, pricing logic, economic guardrails, marketplace monetization rules, and evidence standards that should remain consistent across the website, outreach, proposals, investor materials, and product code.

## 1. Category

Klinikos is an AI-native clinic operating system.

Klinikos is the operating system.
Zumi is the intelligence layer.
Grid is the healthcare network.
EDU is the learning and advancement layer.

The public product must not read like four unrelated businesses or like an ordinary EHR with a chatbot attached.

## 2. The buyer problem

The wedge is the work that breaks between people and systems: unfinished follow-up, referral closure, front-desk handoffs, tasks without owners, missing readiness, fragmented documents, disconnected capacity, revenue-workflow gaps, and poor owner visibility.

The strategic promise is not "replace every healthcare system immediately." It is:

- replace unnecessary standalone software and workarounds when Klinikos can safely own the workflow;
- connect necessary external healthcare infrastructure when it should remain external;
- make Klinikos the operating interface through which the team understands and coordinates the work.

## 3. Three-second comprehension contract

A first-time visitor must be able to answer from the first visible product explanation:

1. What is Klinikos?
2. Who is it for?
3. What problem does it solve?
4. Why is it different from an ordinary EHR?
5. What should I do next?

Public Zumi is proof of the product experience, not a substitute for explaining the product.

## 4. Current clinic commercial anchors

Current code-owned public anchors remain:

- Clinic Operating Analysis: $500 one-time
- Implementation Blueprint: $1,500 one-time
- Founding Clinic Implementation: from $8,000
- Klinikos Core: $995/month
- Klinikos Growth: $1,995/month
- Klinikos Scale: $3,995/month
- Klinikos Enterprise: custom

These numbers are anchors, not evidence that every clinic should pay the same amount. Do not lower or raise them simply to react to a competitor. Validate cost-to-serve, alternative cost, customer value, close rate, implementation burden, and retention before changing the commercial architecture.

## 5. Pricing boundaries

Every internal recommendation should respect three boundaries.

### A. Safe cost floor

Price must cover attributable cost-to-serve and targeted contribution margin.

Cost-to-serve may include, where actually used:

- hosting, compute, database, storage, bandwidth, backup, monitoring, logging;
- AI model input/output, tools, embeddings, search, agent workflows, and voice;
- email, SMS, MMS, phone, fax;
- claims, eligibility, ERA, clearinghouse, eRx, identity, credentials, labs, imaging, telehealth, verification;
- maps/geocoding;
- payment processing, refunds, disputes, payouts;
- onboarding, migration, implementation, support, account management, integration support;
- measurable security/compliance allocation.

Unknown production costs stay UNKNOWN until sourced.

### B. Alternative-cost boundary

Compare Klinikos with the buyer's actual current operating stack, not with one inexpensive EHR license in isolation.

Capture current spend for EHR, practice management, billing, clearinghouse, texting, phone, telehealth, CRM, forms, e-sign, scheduling, payment tools, AI tools, coding, credentialing, fax, tasks, documents, automation, and other meaningful software/workarounds.

### C. Customer-value ceiling

The customer should retain meaningful economic upside. Do not price away the entire savings or operational benefit merely because it can be modeled.

## 6. Evidence classes

Every ROI, savings, and cost model should tag values as one of:

- KNOWN
- SELF_REPORTED
- ESTIMATED
- INFERRED
- UNKNOWN

Do not convert UNKNOWN into a favorable assumption.

## 7. Competitive reality

The market already contains low-price and all-in-one anchors.

Primary-source examples reviewed 2026-08-20:

- Practice Fusion publicly starts at $199/month per provider with a required annual commitment: https://www.practicefusion.com/pricing/
- CharmHealth publicly offers a free limited plan, $0.50/encounter with a $25 monthly minimum, and a $200/provider/month fixed provider plan: https://www.charmhealth.com/ehr/ehr-pricing-us.html
- athenahealth markets athenaOne with pricing tied to a percentage of collections and explicitly positions lower total cost through reducing multiple solutions, relationships, integrations, and technology: https://www.athenahealth.com/solutions/athenaone and https://www.athenahealth.com/why-choose-us/cost-value

Implication: "AI + all-in-one" is not a sufficient category moat. Klinikos must prove continuity, unfinished-work ownership, governed intelligence, operating visibility, Grid network value, and economic evidence.

## 8. Clinic Stack Savings Engine

Build toward an internal and buyer-facing analysis that can calculate:

- current monthly software cost;
- current annual software cost;
- replaceable cost;
- connected/non-replaceable cost;
- Klinikos estimated cost-to-serve;
- proposed Klinikos commercial price;
- estimated customer monthly savings;
- estimated annual savings;
- Klinikos gross profit and gross margin;
- contribution margin;
- implementation cost;
- estimated payback period.

Operational/revenue opportunity may be shown separately only when evidence supports it. Never present recoverable revenue as guaranteed.

## 9. Funnel architecture

Avoid toll-booth psychology.

Preferred sequence:

1. free fit / stack-savings estimate where useful;
2. optional paid Clinic Operating Analysis with real standalone value;
3. implementation proposal for qualified buyers;
4. implementation payment and verified activation;
5. subscription and governed customer-funded usage.

A paid analysis must be useful even if the clinic never buys Klinikos.

## 10. Grid economic law

Liquidity matters first.

- listing/searching/declining should remain low-friction;
- subscription tools can monetize power users and organizations;
- transaction economics may monetize completed value only under an approved server-owned resource-class policy;
- there is no universal Grid transaction percentage approved by this canon.

Resource classes can require different economics and legal review, including:

- space rental;
- equipment;
- nonclinical services;
- staffing;
- regulated clinical services;
- education;
- products;
- organizations;
- professional opportunities.

Potential policy dimensions include resource class, buyer/seller class, jurisdiction, fixed fee, percentage fee, minimum/maximum, processor cost, refund/dispute/payout policy, effective date, version, and legal review status.

Payment never bypasses eligibility, credentialing, clinical authority, privacy, safety, capacity, or healthcare-law constraints.

## 11. Commercial truth

Never fabricate:

- customers;
- testimonials;
- traction;
- revenue;
- clinical outcomes;
- partnerships;
- integrations;
- certifications;
- compliance status;
- FDA status;
- savings;
- competitor prices;
- vendor costs.

Use working product, architecture, truthful workflow demonstrations, security boundaries, accessibility, and real external feedback as proof while early.

## 12. Implementation truth

A buyer should understand what implementation pays for before paying.

Possible components, only when actually deliverable:

- operating analysis;
- tenant/team/RBAC configuration;
- migration planning and supported imports;
- workflow configuration;
- integration planning;
- forms/documents setup;
- staff onboarding;
- Zumi configuration;
- launch support;
- post-launch operating review.

Payment verification does not equal successful implementation.

## 13. Commercial measurement

Optimize for qualified revenue, not vanity traffic.

Key funnel stages:

visitor -> comprehension -> qualified lead -> analysis -> proposal -> close -> activation -> operating value -> retention -> expansion

Useful events include homepage view/CTA, Zumi start/completion, Clinic OS view, Grid/EDU/pricing view, stack analysis start/completion, qualification start/completion, sales contact, checkout start/abandonment, payment verification, onboarding start/completion.

## 14. Market feedback as product evidence

Meaningful outside feedback should be recorded with source, persona, organization, date, surface, feedback, category, severity, evidence, action, status, and resolution.

The August 20, 2026 Caduceus feedback that an evaluator could not discern the value proposition or what the product does is a P0 public-comprehension finding. The website must be able to explain the company without requiring a pitch deck to rescue it.

## 15. Operating law

Every major commercial/product decision should improve one or more of:

- revenue;
- margin;
- retention;
- distribution;
- network effect;
- customer value;
- defensibility;
- security;
- execution speed.

Do not confuse more features with more value, lower pricing with differentiation, AI with a moat, traffic with demand, send count with sales, payment with implementation, or working code with a working business.
