## 5. Capital hierarchy

### A. Customer capital

Best money when available because it creates both cash and market evidence.

Examples:

- paid workflow/operating assessments;
- proof sprints;
- implementation deposits;
- prepaid annual software when commercially appropriate;
- institutional EDU contracts;
- government/workforce contracts;
- paid pilots;
- enterprise implementation deposits.

Customer capital must not be confused with recognized revenue or unrestricted free cash where delivery obligations remain. Accounting treatment stays with Finance/Treasury.

### B. Non-dilutive capital

Pursue when the work genuinely fits the program:

- SBIR/STTR;
- state innovation programs;
- workforce procurement;
- public-sector contracts;
- institutional pilots;
- challenge prizes;
- cloud/startup credits;
- corporate programs.

Do not disguise ordinary SaaS completion as scientific R&D.

### C. Strategic / accelerator / equity capital

Use when the investor/program adds enough capital, distribution, clinical access, payer/provider relationships, enterprise credibility, talent or network leverage to justify dilution.

### D. Debt / business credit

Use when repayment is supportable through operating cash flow, receivables, customer contracts, owner support where appropriate, or a disciplined working-capital plan.

Debt may accelerate proof. Debt must not become a substitute for proof.

### E. Founder / intercompany capital

Document properly. Do not commingle company and personal funds or casually transfer money/IP/revenue between 888 OPS OPES COPIA and Klinikos without an appropriate corporate/accounting basis.

## 6. Capital opportunity scoring

Rank opportunities using a disciplined comparison of:

`PROBABILITY × USEFUL CAPITAL × STRATEGIC VALUE`

against:

`DILUTION + REPAYMENT BURDEN + APPLICATION BURDEN + TIME + DISTRACTION + RISK`.

This is a decision framework, not a mathematically precise valuation formula.

Each Capital Opportunity Register record should explicitly address:

- probability based on current evidence;
- speed to decision;
- speed to cash;
- amount;
- use restrictions;
- dilution;
- repayment burden;
- personal-guarantee/collateral burden;
- strategic distribution/access;
- customer/product validation benefit;
- reporting/administrative burden;
- exclusivity/commitment burden;
- founder time cost;
- whether the opportunity strengthens or distracts from the active customer-value loop.

## 9. Relationship-banking law

A bank relationship is useful evidence and a communication channel. It is not a guaranteed override of underwriting.

Process:

1. choose one primary operating bank relationship based on business needs, fees, treasury tools, startup lending and long-term fit;
2. use the account for legitimate business activity;
3. meet a Small Business Banker / Business Relationship Manager / Commercial Banker;
4. ask how the bank underwrites startups before applying;
5. ask what owner credit, liquidity, deposit relationship, revenue/cash flow, guarantee and documentation it expects;
6. ask whether a preliminary conversation or prequalification creates a hard consumer inquiry;
7. ask what would cause a decline today and what evidence would change the decision;
8. submit only when the application has a defined approval thesis.

Never open accounts or manufacture deposits solely to create a false impression of operating history.

## 10. Personal-credit gate

Because startup lending frequently relies on owner/guarantor underwriting, no serious debt application sequence is approved until the following private data is reviewed through an appropriate channel:

- FICO range(s);
- revolving utilization;
- recent hard inquiries;
- derogatories;
- age of accounts;
- available revolving credit;
- personal debt obligations / DTI where relevant;
- recent new accounts;
- existing guarantees/business debt.

Permitted preparation:

- correct genuine reporting errors;
- pay obligations on time;
- reduce utilization when economically rational;
- avoid unnecessary new accounts/inquiries;
- use legitimate authorized-user relationships only when genuine and actually reported.

Prohibited:

- purchased deceptive tradelines;
- manufactured balances/deposits;
- hidden debt;
- false income;
- false revenue;
- misstated business age/ownership/industry.

## 18. Repayment stress model

Before debt approval, run at minimum:

### Base case

Customer acquisition and implementation occur near the realistic operating plan.

### Conservative case

Sales close more slowly, implementation takes longer, and recurring conversion is delayed.

### Severe downside

New sales materially stall for a defined period.

Debt is acceptable only when there is a credible plan for debt service under a downside case consistent with lender terms and available liquidity/guarantee structure.

Do not assume grants/equity rescue the debt model.

Never use:

`APPLY EVERYWHERE → SEE WHAT HAPPENS`

`UNDERWRITING BOX → SOFT/NON-BINDING DISCOVERY → READINESS GAP → FIX → ONE DELIBERATE APPLICATION → LEARN → NEXT APPLICATION`

For every credit application record in the Lender Readiness Register capture:

- target;
- product;
- amount;
- use of funds;
- application date;
- expected bureau/hard-pull behavior where disclosed;
- personal guarantee;
- relationship requirement;
- eligibility basis;
- expected decision value;
- actual result;
- decline reason when provided;
- next eligible date/action.

- grant scope forces the company away from genuine technical/customer value;
- accelerator exclusivity materially harms the company relative to benefit;
- lender requires uneconomic collateral/guarantees;
- application work displaces higher-probability customer revenue without strategic justification;
