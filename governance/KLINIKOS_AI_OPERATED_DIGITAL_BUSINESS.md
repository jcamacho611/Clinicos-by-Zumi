# KLINIKOS AI-Operated Digital Business Architecture

Status: GOVERNING STRATEGY INPUT
Date: 2026-08-25

## 1. Core idea

The Klinikos website should become an **AI-operated digital business**, not merely a brochure.

A qualified visitor should increasingly be able to:

`DISCOVER → UNDERSTAND → SELF-IDENTIFY → QUALIFY → SEE A PERSONALIZED OPERATING MAP → ASK QUESTIONS → RECEIVE THE RIGHT NEXT STEP/OFFER → ACCEPT TERMS → PAY OR REQUEST CONTRACTING → SCHEDULE ONBOARDING → RECEIVE FOLLOW-UP → ENTER KLINIKOS`

with minimal manual founder involvement.

The AI does not get unrestricted authority. It operates within explicit business, legal, clinical, privacy, pricing and financial gates.

## 2. Public Zumi as the front door

The repository already contains a public Zumi control that:

- operates on public routes
- uses server-side intelligence
- routes users to relevant public destinations
- refuses private patient-record requests
- refuses diagnosis/prescribing requests
- refuses disclosure of private prompts, source code, pricing/margin logic and security heuristics
- degrades honestly when intelligence is unavailable
- suggests role/goal-specific next questions

Extend this into a governed digital sales/education concierge rather than creating a separate chatbot product.

## 3. Digital business operating loop

### Stage A — Acquisition

AI and deterministic systems support:

- search-intent landing pages
- useful SEO content
- Operating Map
- role-specific education
- Grid/EDU discovery
- social/referral campaign destinations
- source attribution

### Stage B — Comprehension

Public Zumi determines, from user-confirmed information:

- who the visitor is
- whether they own/manage a practice
- their operational problem
- their current goal
- what Klinikos surface can help

Never infer regulated credentials from self-description.

### Stage C — Qualification

Collect only appropriate non-PHI business information such as:

- organization type
- locations
- approximate staff/provider scale
- current software categories
- operational pain
- workflow volume ranges
- desired outcome
- implementation urgency
- procurement needs

Do not ask public visitors for patient-identifying or clinical information.

### Stage D — Personalized Operating Map

Create a plain-English map of the organization's stated workflow, for example:

`CALL → BOOKING → INTAKE → VISIT → FOLLOW-UP → BILLING`

Highlight potential gaps based only on supplied information.

Show:

- what may be unfinished
- why it matters
- what Klinikos can handle
- what currently remains external
- what next step is recommended

Do not fabricate ROI.

### Stage E — Offer selection

The AI may explain approved offers from the governed Offer Registry.

It may not invent prices, discounts, guarantees or contract terms.

Offer logic should consider:

- buyer type
- problem
- scale
- geography
- required implementation
- product maturity
- current approved pricing experiment

Outputs may be:

- free triage
- paid analysis
- proof sprint
- implementation conversation
- subscription starting point
- enterprise sales path
- Grid/EDU enrollment path

### Stage F — Contracting

For self-service/low-risk offers:

- show current scope
- current price
- applicable terms
- acceptance evidence
- checkout

For enterprise/custom work:

- collect qualification data
- prepare draft proposal/SOW from approved templates
- route for human review when terms are non-standard
- schedule buyer call or procurement handoff

AI cannot bind Klinikos to non-approved contract terms.

### Stage G — Payment

Use Financial OS truth:

`OFFER → CHECKOUT INTENT → PROCESSOR → PAYMENT EVIDENCE → RECONCILIATION → ENTITLEMENT/DELIVERY`

Never treat browser redirect as payment.

### Stage H — Automated follow-up

Zumi/CRM can trigger governed follow-up based on real state:

- incomplete Operating Map
- completed analysis without purchase
- checkout started but not verified
- proposal delivered
- no response
- paid customer awaiting onboarding
- onboarding incomplete

Respect consent, suppression, frequency and channel rules.

### Stage I — Onboarding

After verified commercial activation:

- create organization/account workflow
- request only required setup information
- guide locations/staff/services/imports/integrations
- schedule implementation work
- provide training
- surface blockers

Zumi should turn onboarding into a checklist/operating stage, not a long email thread.

### Stage J — Expansion

Once customer value is proven, surface relevant expansion based on actual product usage or unmet need:

- Revenue OS
- Zumi automation
- Grid
- additional locations
- EDU
- Network
- enterprise governance
- additional integrations

Do not spam every customer with every module.

## 4. AI digital roles

Klinikos may use specialized governed agents/services internally, even if the user experiences one Zumi identity.

### AI Webmaster

Responsibilities:

- detect broken public routes
- stale copy
- missing metadata
- inconsistent CTAs
- broken forms
- page-speed regressions
- accessibility regressions
- stale product claims

May prepare fixes. Production changes still require release policy.

### AI SEO/Content Operator

Responsibilities:

- maintain buyer-intent map
- discover legitimate search topics
- prepare useful content
- identify internal-link opportunities
- detect stale pages
- maintain schema/meta suggestions

Cannot mass-publish low-quality programmatic SEO or unverified medical/commercial claims.

### AI Sales Concierge

Responsibilities:

- identify visitor role/goal
- explain Klinikos
- qualify organization
- prepare Operating Map
- route to approved offer
- answer objections using approved truth
- create sales record
- schedule follow-up

Cannot invent traction, customer logos, ROI, pricing or legal promises.

### AI Proposal Assistant

Responsibilities:

- create proposal/SOW draft from approved templates
- insert verified buyer data
- scope known services
- calculate approved pricing
- prepare comparison/savings model when evidence exists

Non-standard legal/economic terms require human approval.

### AI CRM Operator

Responsibilities:

- create/update lead record
- maintain stage
- summarize interaction
- schedule next action
- detect orphan leads
- prepare follow-up
- identify buyer objections

CRM state must reflect actual events.

### AI Customer Success Operator

Responsibilities:

- onboarding reminders
- first-value tracking
- training guidance
- adoption gaps
- customer-value evidence
- renewal/expansion readiness

### AI Support Triage

Responsibilities:

- answer public/product documentation questions
- classify support issue
- collect safe diagnostics
- route to engineering/security/clinical/privacy/commercial owner

Support AI must not expose other tenants or provide clinical care.

### AI Business Analyst

Responsibilities:

- website funnel
- conversion
- pricing experiments
- customer objections
- feature adoption
- customer value
- cost/margin
- Grid liquidity
- EDU outcomes

It recommends. Governed decision makers approve consequential changes.

## 5. Autonomy levels for the online business

### L0 Observe

Read metrics/state and explain.

### L1 Recommend

Suggest page/offer/follow-up changes.

### L2 Prepare

Draft copy, email, proposal, page changes, SEO changes, CRM updates.

### L3 Execute after approval

Send a non-routine proposal, publish material claims, change pricing, launch major campaigns, alter contracts or deploy consequential product changes only after appropriate approval.

### L4 Authorized autopilot

Low-risk repeatable actions may be pre-authorized, such as:

- transactional follow-up based on actual funnel state
- bounce/suppression handling
- safe metadata regeneration
- internal CRM state updates
- scheduling reminders
- content freshness alerts
- support triage
- routine onboarding reminders

### L5 Prohibited autonomy

AI never independently:

- changes corporate ownership
- issues equity
- enters non-approved financing
- signs non-approved contracts
- invents or changes official pricing
- promises healthcare outcomes
- creates false customer claims
- releases PHI
- diagnoses/prescribes
- makes regulated professional decisions
- moves money outside approved deterministic/payment rules

## 6. Website route model

The public website should progressively support:

- `/` category + wedge + primary CTA
- `/how-it-works`
- `/operating-map`
- `/capabilities`
- `/ecosystem`
- `/care` / Current Visit product story
- `/grid`
- `/edu`
- `/revenue`
- `/intelligence`
- `/enterprise`
- `/trust`
- `/pricing` or approved starting-points page
- `/start`
- `/sales`
- `/about`
- `/contact`
- `/resources`
- `/use-cases/...`
- `/specialties/...` where content is substantial
- Terms/Privacy/Accessibility

Reconcile with actual route registry before adding/renaming routes.

## 7. Frontend speech law

The site must apply the same plain-English law as the product.

Every public page should answer:

1. What can Klinikos do for me?
2. Why does that matter?
3. What does it replace/connect?
4. What should I do next?

Use professional terminology only after plain-language explanation.

## 8. AI-generated interface, not AI-generated essays

When possible, Zumi should materialize:

- Operating Map
- software replacement plan
- stack-cost comparison
- readiness checklist
- tailored product path
- pricing/offer card
- scheduling options
- onboarding checklist
- Grid opportunity view
- EDU path

instead of returning long prose.

## 9. Digital revenue automation

The online business should connect:

`TRAFFIC → INTENT → ZUMI → QUALIFICATION → OPERATING MAP → OFFER → TERMS → VERIFIED PAYMENT/CONTRACT → ONBOARDING → FIRST VALUE → EXPANSION`

Every step needs analytics and evidence.

## 10. AI-operated experimentation

Maintain controlled experiments for:

- headline
- CTA
- Operating Map question order
- offer framing
- proof placement
- persona route
- pricing experiment when officially approved
- onboarding sequence

Guardrails:

- minimum sample/evidence threshold
- no clinical/privacy/security degradation
- no automated official pricing changes without approval
- no dark patterns
- no fake scarcity
- no fabricated social proof

## 11. Website reliability automation

Continuously monitor:

- public uptime
- route health
- forms
- checkout links
- API/Zumi health
- SEO indexability
- sitemap
- broken links
- performance
- accessibility
- analytics pipeline
- transactional email delivery
- payment webhook health

Create actionable incidents rather than silent failures.

## 12. Founder dashboard

The founder/leadership view should eventually answer:

- How many qualified visitors today?
- What are they trying to solve?
- Which pages/CTAs convert?
- How many Operating Maps completed?
- How many offers generated?
- How many verified payments/contracts?
- What revenue entered?
- What leads need human intervention?
- What objections are recurring?
- What feature demand is emerging?
- What AI actions were taken automatically?
- What AI actions are awaiting approval?
- What is broken?
- What should we do next?

The goal is that humans manage exceptions and strategy while AI handles repeat online operations.

## 13. Human-in-the-loop business gates

Humans remain responsible for:

- official pricing approval
- non-standard discounts
- material contract changes
- legal representations
- enterprise commitments
- regulated/privacy decisions
- investor/equity commitments
- high-risk communications
- major product/brand changes

The correct system is not "AI can do anything." It is "AI can do everything repetitive that has clear policy, evidence and safe boundaries."

## 14. Final target

The online portion of Klinikos should increasingly operate like a 24/7 digital company:

- market educates itself
- Zumi understands visitors
- qualified leads are captured automatically
- useful personalized analysis is generated automatically
- approved offers are presented automatically
- payments and contracting state are reconciled automatically
- onboarding is orchestrated automatically
- follow-up happens automatically
- support is triaged automatically
- SEO/site health is continuously reviewed
- leadership sees exceptions, decisions and economic results

Humans focus on strategy, high-value sales, customer relationships, regulated authority, enterprise negotiations and product direction.
